# Declaring current attention — the focus kind

A focus records what the project is advancing right now and who carries it — an involvement declaration. It is a decision — something committed to, not something noticed: the project commits its present attention, and the record makes visible what otherwise lives only in heads — the current work, who is engaged with each piece, how long this holds. Its reader arrives asking exactly that: what is being driven, who carries each piece and what waits for pickup, and what ends this period — and a good focus is the shortest record that answers all three.

**A focus points at work; it does not contain it.** Its targets are existing entries — most often plans and decisions — that the project is actually driving in this period; the focus names them, and their content stays theirs. Engagement and dates live in the focus, never on the target: the target stays usable in every later period, and the chain of foci over time records how attention moved. A focus stands on its involvements — references and topic labels are optional, and where present they carry grounding, not the work.

**Who, what, and when live in the structure.** Each involvement joins one target with the people engaged on it and the window it runs in. People and a window stated once for the whole focus are defaults every involvement inherits; an involvement carries its own only where it genuinely differs — its own people when engagement splits across targets, its own window when a piece runs on a different clock, not a restatement of the shared one. And engagement is plural by design: several people can be angled at one target, because that is how work actually distributes — the kind has no single-owner slot. In a timber-framing shop, "through November the shop is attending to the barn-frame commission: Mara drives the joinery layout, and the client walkthrough runs from the 20th" is the shape — one period, several pieces, each carried by whoever actually carries it.

**Open for pickup is a statement.** An involvement can deliberately name no one: the target is in scope for this period and waiting for whoever takes it up — in the same shop, "the timber-sourcing decision is open for whoever picks it up." That differs from saying nothing, which merely inherits the shared default; the deliberate blank is first-class, and reaching for it beats dropping the target or guessing an owner. A focus made entirely of open-for-pickup targets reads two ways — a deliberate pickup board, or a declaration still missing its people — so its body says which.

**What the period sets down is the half only dialogue produces.** Naming what the project attends to is easy and mostly uncontested — the active work names itself. Agreeing what waits is the half that needs the people in the room, because setting something down costs someone something: a commitment they carry stalls, a piece they wanted in this period does not make it. That negotiation is where a focus's why comes from, and it is the one part a later reader cannot reconstruct from the target list. A focus written without that conversation reads as a list of everything — which is no focus at all. So the body names what was deliberately left out and why, and a missing candidate reads as a choice, not an oversight.

**The body carries why now.** The structure answers who, what, and when; the body carries the period's narrative: what this stretch of work is about, why these targets over the other open commitments, and how they relate — what ships first, what unblocks what, what runs in parallel. It also states what would close the focus in the project's own terms, functionally where it can: met when the thing works, not merely when it exists.

**Honest about the window.** A window with a start and no end says the true thing most periods can say: this began now and has no fixed finish. An end date belongs on a genuinely bounded push — a stretch with a real finish line. And when even the pace is undetermined, the honest move is no window at all, with the body saying the work proceeds as time permits. The window is a claim like any other: state what is actually known about the period, not what the form seems to invite.

**Two ways out, and the drafter makes the call.** A focus is not work to be completed: it is a standing declaration of current attention, replaced when priorities change — exactly why it is its own kind rather than a dispatched piece of work. It ends superseded by the focus that replaces it when priorities shift mid-period, or closed by a done signal when the period ran its course; and like any entry it can be retired by whatever entry carries the reasoning why it stopped holding. Nothing arbitrates between the paths — the call belongs to whoever writes the ending record, deliberately. And a period may close without its targets completing — attention moved on, and that is legitimate — but the closing record says so rather than leaving the shortfall to be inferred.

**Replacement and coexistence each say so.** A focus that supersedes another names what changed — which finish line was overtaken, and why the new shape displaced the old. A replacement sharing no targets with its predecessor is a wholesale shift of priorities, and it reads as an oversight unless the body names it as one. Parallel foci are legitimate — a project can attend to two strands at once — so a new focus that does not replace an existing one says so and records the neighbor as related; silence reads as displacement.

**The layer sets the reach, and foci can nest.** No layer is pinned. A focus at a higher layer reaches further and stays less specific — a broad direction of attention holding over many months; a lower one is shorter and more concrete, naming particular work and the particular people on it. Most land tactical, because arranging current work is tactical. And foci nest: a broader period holds narrower ones inside it, each with its own window, and a narrower focus reaching its end does not end the one containing it.
