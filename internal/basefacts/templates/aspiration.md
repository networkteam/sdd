# Orienting the work — the aspiration kind

An aspiration records a direction the work is pulled toward: not a target the project will reach, a quality it keeps moving toward over time. It is a decision — something committed to, not something noticed: the project chooses which way its work should lean, and later work is read against that choice. Its reader arrives asking two things — which way does this pull, and how do I tell whether a piece of work moves with it or against it — and a good aspiration is the shortest record that lets someone who was not in the room run that check. Setting direction for the whole is strategic thinking almost by nature, and most aspirations name the strategic layer.

**A pull that binds nothing.** An aspiration exercises pull: it tells the work which way to go without binding any single piece of it. No task conforms to it, and none can violate it — for a village bakery, "every loaf we sell was baked that morning from grain whose farm we can name" leans buying, baking, and hiring in one direction without obliging any one delivery, and no inspection could pass or fail a loaf against it. And the direction carries no completion gate: it outlives every piece of work aligned with it, which is why it stays a live commitment rather than an item that could be worked off a list.

**Alignment is a degree, never a verdict.** Asking "does this comply?" of an aspiration is a category error; the question is "how well does this align?", and the answer is a position on a gradient, not a pass or a fail. Two consequences follow. A detour is not a violation: a step that moves obliquely, even away for a stretch, is coherent as long as it is explicable against the direction. And an aspiration never gates work: when a proposal strains against a pull, the tension is material for dialogue, never grounds to block.

**One pull among several.** A project holds several aspirations at once, and pulls that strain against each other — for a coffee roastery, growth against brand purity, reach against depth of relationship — are the normal case, not a defect: together they form a field of tensions rather than one target, and each tension is resolved case by case in the work. A new aspiration therefore positions itself against the pulls it will live beside: where it reinforces one or runs on an unrelated axis, nothing needs defending; where it strains against an active pull, the entry says so and argues the coexistence — writing the tension in is the craft, leaving it unspoken is the fault.

**The pull is argued, never derived.** No amount of reading the record produces an aspiration: it says what the people involved want the work to become, and the only place that gets settled is between them. So the grounding rule bends here by design — an aspiration may synthesize direction the record does not yet hold, because demanding that every strategic pull trace back to something already written down prevents strategic thinking: ground what can be grounded, and own the rest as the project's reach. What the entry owes in exchange is the argument itself: what understanding of the project the direction rests on, what was weighed on the way to it — summarized in the body, with a pointer to a fuller articulation where one exists, rather than a restatement of it. An aim nobody can say why the project wants is an aim nobody holds — and because aspirations sit in tension with each other by design, a pull whose reasoning was never had will not survive its first conflict with another one: tension is resolved by argument, not by lookup, and an entry carrying no argument brings nothing to that table.

**Concrete enough to score against.** The direction opens as a plain declarative sentence in the present tense, stated as if it were already the project's nature. The rest of the body makes it checkable: name what the pull pushes against — the habits and counter-forces it exists to resist — and give a working test where one exists (for a coffee roastery, "for any new offering: does it bring the drinker closer to the grower?"). A framing toward something and a framing away from something are equally valid — sometimes the negative framing carries more energy. The measure of the whole entry is one line: a later decision must be scorable against it by someone who was not there.

**The check when the record runs out.** Together, the active aspirations are the surface the project's movement is checked against for direction — and the preparation for dialogue: in a novel situation the record does not back, the proposal is weighed against them, asking which pulls it moves toward, which away from, and where the tension sits. A guideline could not hold this seat without collapsing the direction check into a compliance check. The role sets two demands: a pull too vague to score against contributes nothing at the moment the check runs, and the active set stays small — few enough that whoever runs the check holds every pull at once.

**Never completed — retired with its why.** Aspirations shift when the underlying understanding of what the project is doing shifts, which makes them the slowest-moving decisions — but they are not fixed doctrine: a pull is extended over time and changes when a misalignment is observed. What never happens is completion: no act of work reaches a state in which the pull is fulfilled, so nothing completes an aspiration, and retirement is its only exit — superseded by the evolved direction that replaces it, or closed by whatever entry carries why the direction stopped holding. The stated rationale is what qualifies the retirement, not the kind carrying it.

**Confidence tracks how far the pull has proved itself.** A new direction may honestly start low and rise as the work repeatedly aligns with it and that alignment proves useful. A high-confidence aspiration also carries more force in dialogue: work pulling against a well-proven direction is a sign something is off, worth examining rather than waving through.
