# Marking an unknown — the question kind

A question records an acknowledged unknown: something the project does not know and needs to. It is not an observation and not a commitment — a marker of uncertainty — and it is a signal: something noticed, not something committed to. Open questions sit beside the open gaps on the project's working surface, showing where attention is owed. A question's reader arrives asking three things — what exactly is not known, what keeps it unsettled, and what would resolve it — and a good question is the shortest record that answers all three.

**The unknown itself leads.** Open with the unknown phrased as a question — an interrogative sharp enough to be read on its own, without the occasion that raised it. The framing is more than style: whoever later answers the question must be able to name it by what it asks, so the entry poses the unknown itself, not merely its topic area — and a claimed resolution is checked against the question's own words. An unknown posed too broadly for any answer to demonstrably settle it fails its purpose at the moment of writing.

**Name the occasion that raised it.** Questions arrive out of concrete work — a review that surfaced an ambiguity, a piece of work accepted with a doubt attached, a customer's ask nobody could answer. Say where this one came from and point at the entry that raised it. The occasion and the live tension are what the question carries in from the world; candidate answers can be re-reasoned later, but where the unknown came from cannot be reconstructed once forgotten.

**Recorded because deciding is not ripe.** A question is captured deliberately in place of a commitment: the project is not ready to decide, and says so — the entry sits in the graph waiting for more data or the conversation that resolves it. Recording the unknown is a hedge against deciding by omission: an unasked question gets answered anyway, silently, by whatever the work happens to do. And a question captured on purpose gives later design its starting point — the deciding, when it comes, begins from a stated unknown instead of rediscovering it.

**State the tension that keeps it open.** An unknown reads as genuinely unsettled only when the entry says what pulls each way — the consideration that makes one answer attractive and the cost that makes it doubtful. Without the tension, the entry reads as merely unasked, and a later reader cannot tell whether it awaits real resolution or never got five minutes of thought.

**Explore without committing.** A question may walk the solution space: candidate answers, directions worth trying, criteria an answer would be judged by — all may enter, each marked as what it is, an unsettled candidate rather than the answer. The entry stays a signal exactly as long as it declines to commit, and may say so outright. The moment it picks a route — an imperative with an owner and a timeline — it is a decision in a signal's clothes, and the choice belongs in its own entry.

**Face the answer that will come.** When the shape of resolution is already partly visible, record it. Name what any answer must keep intact or would strain against — "any answer must keep the tasting schedule workable for a two-person shop" — so a later decision that conflicts with it is visible as a conflict, not an oversight. And when the path to an answer is known — a trial, a measurement, an unknown answerable only through a real attempt — name the move that would settle it.

**One unknown, with its faces named.** The unit is the unknown, not the theme: two questions that different resolutions would answer are two entries. But one unknown can have strands — several angles that stand or fall with the same resolution — and those travel together in one entry, each stated, none silently absorbed. When a question is one face of a larger unknown the graph already holds, it says so and points at its siblings — mirroring a recorded concern from the opposite angle — so the answering work sees the whole shape.

**How a question resolves.** It stays open until something answers it: most often a decision — the answer is a choice, recorded with its why — or knowledge alone, when a fact or insight dissolves it by answering it and no choice was required. Learning that the timber yard stocks only one grade dissolves "which grades can we source?" without anyone deciding anything. A decision may also close a question that will not be pursued, carrying the why. Sharpening a still-open unknown is supersession by a refined question, not closure. What the record owes its answer is traceability, not certification: the answering entry can be checked against the question's framing, but nothing adjudicates whether the answer is right. And a question never closes anything itself: asking is not answering.

**Choosing question at all.** The neighbor tests, each a single question. Does the entry assert something the project now knows, or mark something it does not? "A courier ships a 500-gram package for under six euros" informs — a fact; "what should shipping cost the customer?" marks — a question. Is there an expectation already held that the world fails to meet? Then it is a gap — a question lacks the expected side itself, which is why knowledge alone may answer a question but never close a gap. Does the entry connect what is already recorded, leaving nothing owed? An insight — "children settle faster when handover happens at the door" owes nothing; "does handover work better at the door or in the room?" owes an answer.
