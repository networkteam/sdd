# Binding an actor to their part — the role kind

A role commits one named participant to a shape of contribution on this project: this actor participates as this. It is a decision — something committed to, not something noticed — because a part is granted and relied on: from here on, the project routes work and dialogue as if it holds. Its reader arrives asking three things — whose part is this, what do they hold here, and when does something come to them — and a good role is the shortest record that answers all three.

**The actor comes first.** A role binds a participant the graph already knows: capture the actor, then the role. A role never introduces a person — it points at one — and it binds to the actor's stable name, not to any single entry, so a later refinement of the actor's record — a corrected spelling, a sharpened description — leaves every role standing exactly as written. A role is never rewritten to track an identity fix. And when one conversation yields both halves — who someone is and what they do here — that is two entries drafted together, actor and role; deferring the role half is a stated choice, never a silent drop of what was volunteered.

**Shape enough to route by.** The substance of the entry is the participant's specialization and preferred contribution — the authority they hold, the decisions that are theirs, what is delegated away and what must come to them — stated clearly enough that another participant can reason about when to bring work or dialogue to this person. "Ana holds sourcing and the roast profiles: she selects and approves every green lot, sets profile changes, and purchases over the standing limit go through her — day-to-day roasting runs without her" can be routed against; "Ana contributes to the roastery" is a role in form only. Naming what runs without the person carries as much shape as naming what waits on them. And the shape belongs to one person: a role scopes exactly one named participant, and a participant may hold several roles at once when the parts genuinely differ.

**The weight carried here, not the background.** The recorded confusion between role and actor is expertise, so the boundary deserves stating outright: what a participant brings from outside — training, years in a trade, a qualification — is actor material; the weight that background carries in this project is the role. "Trained cupper, twenty years buying green coffee" describes the person; "every lot ships on her approval" describes the part. When drafting a role pulls toward biography, that sentence belongs in the actor entry, and the role keeps only the authority the background grounds.

**Standing, not frozen.** A role records the stable part — what this person usually holds, not what they happen to be doing in the current stretch — yet standing does not mean permanent. When the participation genuinely shifts — the scope widens or narrows, responsibilities move to other hands, the specialization grows into new territory — a refined role supersedes the old one, the same way any decision retires when it no longer matches the commitment it recorded.

**It reads whole on its own.** The prose introduces the bound name itself — "Jonas carries layout and joinery decisions on site" opens with Jonas — so the record makes sense read alone, and the first sentence stands alone because overview surfaces lead with it. The part is described in the project's own words, not picked from a menu of standard titles: there is no fixed vocabulary of roles, and inventing one adds classification without adding shape. And the body speaks about the person and the part, nothing else — rules about how entries link or validate belong to the machinery, not to a role's prose.

**The person's exit retires the parts.** A role's ending is mostly derived, and that is its distinguishing lifecycle property. When the bound actor's identity closes — the person leaves the project — every role bound to it retires with it, and no separate closing entries are owed; conversely, a correction or refinement of the actor's record is not a departure, and the roles ride through untouched. A directive may still close a single role directly — the part withdrawn while the person stays — but that is the unusual path: most role endings are either the participation shifting, which is supersession, or the person going, which the actor's own closure already carries.

**Choosing role at all.** The neighbor tests the overview does not run, each a single question. Does the sentence stay true with a different name in it? Then it is a guideline binding everyone — a guiding directive — not a role: "material substitutions are confirmed before ordering" holds whoever orders; "material substitutions come to Jonas before order" does not. Is the attention bounded to the present period? Then it is a focus — "Marta covers the toddler group this month" focuses; "Marta holds the settling-in period for new children, first contact for a family's first six weeks" is her standing part. The deciding test against the actor kind — outside versus here — lives in the type-system introduction; pull it there.

**Honesty in the small fields.** The layer is process, whatever depth the person's own work reaches: a role is about how this project works — who holds what — not about the work itself. Confidence may honestly sit below certain, because a role is an evolving commitment about participation, not a settled observation, and shapes drift as projects do. Topic labels are rarely owed — the named participant and the part already carry the subject.
