# Harvested neighbour tests — raw material for the discrimination fact

Per `20260818-133146-d-cpt-fpm`, kind discrimination is factored out of the per-kind
authoring facts into one dedicated fact referenced from the type-system introduction.
These are the tests the six rewritten kinds would have carried in a "Choosing X at all"
paragraph, returned separately instead of written into the documents.

**Still to harvest:** the choosing paragraphs of the eight already-shipped facts —
done, procedure, gap, directive, insight, fact, question, plan — which must be removed
from those documents as part of the same work. Watch for reciprocal pairs when
assembling: directive/plan, activity/directive, actor/role and focus/role each state
one test from both sides.

## actor

- **vs role (frame-relativity)** — Is the description a position held in another frame? In a parish project, "CEO of her company" or "member of the parish council" is identity here even though it is a role there: what someone is elsewhere grounds who they are, and only what they do within this project is a role. (The base outside-vs-here test lives in the introduction.)
- **vs a plain participants mention** — Is the name just someone who took part once? In a child-care centre, a visiting inspector quoted in one meeting's minutes is recorded by listing them among that entry's participants; the actor entry is for a name the graph will attribute work to from now on. And even for a declared participant, routine participation never warrants pointing an entry at the actor itself — the listed name already carries the reference.
- **vs fact** — Is the entry an observation about a person rather than the person's identity? In a roastery, "the new apprentice holds a pastry certification from Lyon" is knowledge about the world; making the apprentice a name the graph attributes work to is the actor. NOTE: unsourced beyond the shipped fact-kind line; ruled in during review.

## role

- **vs guiding directive** — Does the sentence stay true with a different name in it? In timber framing, "material substitutions are confirmed before ordering" holds whoever orders — a guiding directive; "material substitutions come to Jonas before order" does not — a role.
- **vs focus** — Is the attention bounded to the present period? In child care, "Marta covers the toddler group this month" focuses; "Marta holds the settling-in period for new children, first contact for a family's first six weeks" is her standing part. Capture-side form: ask "is that your usual part, or just this stretch's work?" — a single period's pattern is not yet a role.
- **vs actor** — the deciding test, outside versus here, lives in the introduction. What role adds past it is the expertise instantiation, which stays in the role fact as semantics: background brought from outside is actor material, the weight it carries here is the role.

## activity

- **vs plan** — the deciding test, WHAT versus THAT, lives in the introduction. Instantiation if wanted: in timber framing, "the frame is done when all twelve bents are raised and every joint inspected" defines what must be true — a plan; "raise the four bents on the east wall this week" dispatches — an activity.
- **vs directive** — Is a route being chosen, with a why to carry? In timber framing, "load-bearing joints are pegged, not screwed" chose among ways — a directive; "peg the joints on the east wall bents" sends work already ruled on its way — an activity.
  - Rider 1 (lossy direction): an entry carrying a non-obvious choice inside the dispatch stays a directive — a why lost crossing that boundary cannot be recovered; ambiguous cases stay directives.
  - Rider 2 (work-shape trap): read the shape of the work, not of its outputs — in a roastery, "run Thursday's cupping with the new roaster" is an activity even though which lots make the cut is unknowable in advance. The shipped directive fact carries the mirror of this with a child-care handover example.
- **vs done** — Has the work already happened? In a roastery, "roasted and cupped the new lot" records — a done; "roast and cup the new lot" commits — an activity.
- **vs focus** — Is the commitment about the present period, naming who attends to what, rather than one piece of work? In child care, "through March, Jun carries the settling-in observations" is a focus; "write the March settling-in notes" is an activity.
- **vs procedure** — Will the same work run again and again by the same steps? In a roastery, "the weekly cupping review" runs repeatedly — a procedure; "this week's cupping review" happens once — an activity.

## focus

- **vs directive** — Is this standing direction over an area rather than the present period's attention? In timber framing, "load-bearing joints are pegged, not screwed" keeps holding after November; the barn-frame commission does not. (The shipped directive fact carries this from its side — complement, don't mirror.)
- **vs plan** — Does the entry itself enumerate what must be true when the work is finished? In timber framing, the plan for the barn frame is a target of the focus, never the focus itself, and its outcomes stay in it.
- **vs activity** — Is one piece of work being dispatched to be completed? A focus is a standing declaration of current attention, replaced when priorities change, which is precisely the lifecycle a dispatched piece of work cannot have.
- **vs role** — Is this what a person contributes as a standing matter rather than what they attend to right now? In a childcare group, "Ana handles parent communication" is a role bound to the actor; "this month Ana is driving the new settling-in flow" belongs in a focus.

## aspiration

- **vs directive** — the deciding test, force not completion, lives in the introduction. Supporting pair on the pull side: for a village bakery, "every loaf we sell was baked that morning from grain whose farm we can name" pulls; "nothing leaves the shop without a second person's sign-off" pushes.
  - GUARD: the absence of a completion criterion cannot pick between them — both kinds may lack one by design. An earlier version of this test was discredited on exactly that ground and still ships on three legacy surfaces awaiting reconciliation.
- **vs focus** — Does the commitment hold only for the present period, naming who attends to what? For a village bakery, "this quarter the bakery attends to the wholesale accounts, with Rosa on ordering" is a focus; the grain-provenance pull outlives every quarter.
- **vs plan** — Can what-counts-as-done be enumerated and checked item by item? For the same bakery, "the new oven is in when it passes gas inspection and completes a 200-loaf trial bake" plans; a pull never reaches a checked box.
- Deliberately NOT offered — vs contract: a historical neighbour, and the introduction owns that the kind takes no new entries.

## annotation

- **The strip-the-references test (vs every narrative kind)** — Strip the references away: is anything left still worth reading? If yes it is not an annotation. In a timber shop, "the shop knows exactly as much as before; it just could not find the three entries in one pull" marks an annotation; "deliveries keep arriving below spec" is a gap, because something new is claimed and something is owed. This is the strongest test of the set.
- **The attention test (vs focus)** — Does the entry commit attention — who attends to what, for this period? In a child-care group, "this month the team watches settling-in at handover" is a focus; a label laid over last term's handover entries is an annotation.
