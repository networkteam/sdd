# Deciding how to go forward — the directive kind

A directive records a decision on how to go forward: it acts on what was noticed, chooses among routes, and sometimes overturns an earlier decision and changes direction. For most newcomers it is the kind without a familiar home — in most working records the decision itself lives between the lines, in documents, meeting notes, or memory — and recording it is how the project stays transparent about its why: a future reader finds what was decided, which alternatives were weighed, and what carried the choice. It is a picture of the current understanding, not a law — new input can change it. What it obliges afterwards is named by its `intent`, and the three postures behave almost like three kinds sharing one form.

**The choice carries its why.** A directive is readable as a choice made: what was decided, and the reasoning that carried it — grounded in the entries it reasons from, every one of them referenced, and committing nothing the grounding does not support. Most directives are born in dialogue, and the dialogue's material must stay retrievable from the entry — the arguments raised, the alternatives weighed and set aside — summarized in the body, with an attachment carrying the detail when there is much of it. When a directive retires or replaces an earlier commitment, the body says what changed in the world or the thinking — restating what the retired entry said is not a rationale.

**Intent is chosen, never defaulted.** Three questions settle it. Does something still have to happen because of this? Then `pending` — the commitment has a horizon, and a reader can say what fulfills it. It need not decide every detail on the way there: a pending directive is carried out through later plans, gaps, and decisions, each with its own dialogue — what it must carry is the direction and the why those detail decisions will lean on. Is it a guideline that keeps applying, rather than a target that could be completed? Then `guiding`. Was the deciding the whole of it — considered, ruled, and finished in the ruling? Then `settled`, and the body must say why nothing follows, because that stated why is all that separates deliberately terminal from silently dropped. There is deliberately no default: the posture is not derivable from the text, so a default would fabricate it. And no check verifies the fit between the declared intent and the narrative's shape — a pending directive that reads as perpetual orientation, or a guiding one that reads as a bounded task, is caught at drafting or not at all.

**A guiding directive binds.** A guideline is not decoration: work and later entries are expected to conform to it, and a violation is observable — "nothing leaves the shop without a second person's sign-off" can be broken and seen broken. A stance nobody could violate pulls instead of pushes, and belongs among the aspirations. Yet a guiding directive is not mechanically followable like a checklist: it carries the why an area's day-to-day constraints derive from — "accessibility governs all design work, because we must comply" is the reasoning that narrower, checkable working rules hang under. Guiding directives are meant to stay few and broad — a guideline covers an area; a constraint that fires at a recognizable recurring moment is a narrower thing living under it, not another guideline beside it.

**Retirement follows the posture.** A pending directive closes like work: the act that delivers it closes it, a later directive may close it with the reasoning why nothing will be built, or a replacement supersedes it. A guiding directive is not completed by work — it is superseded by the guideline that replaces it, or closed by a directive explaining why it no longer holds; a done closing a guideline is a category error. A settled directive only supersedes. Reclassifying a directive that hardened into something else is supersession too, never an edit. And the directive is the graph's universal retiring instrument: it may close any decision — and the stable signal kinds — carrying the why in its body.

**Refining without replacing.** A directive may sharpen an active plan in place — adding to what the plan must satisfy while the plan stays untouched and both retire together. That augmenting shape is first-class: reaching for supersession to express a refinement fabricates a successor that does not exist.
