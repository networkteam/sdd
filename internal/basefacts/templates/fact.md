# Recording discovered knowledge — the fact kind

A fact records discovered knowledge: something learned about the world that informs later work without itself demanding any — the reference material a project looks up and cites. It is a signal — something noticed, not something committed to. Its reader arrives asking three things — what is claimed, why it can be trusted, and where the fuller record lives — and a good fact is the shortest record that answers all three.

**The claim carries how it was reached.** A fact is written so a later reader can re-derive it: the source it rests on — a document, a supplier's quote, what a customer said — and, where the claim was worked out rather than read off, the method behind it, dated when the date matters. Verification is not the price of entry: a claim genuinely hard to check is still recorded, named as such and graded honestly in confidence, because knowledge the project holds is worth more in the record than out of it. What is never acceptable is a claim with nothing behind it. And it is stated in the project's own vocabulary, in exact words; when the knowledge arrives through another entry, borrowing that entry's words conveys the relationship, never imports its content.

**The claim in the body, the record at its source.** The figure is often the claim itself — "the drum roaster holds twelve kilograms per batch" is nothing without its number — so the discriminator is work, not durability: a number stays out only when it is residue of the checking, binding every later reader to reconcile it. Past the claim, evidence scales its home: a small observation sits inline, dated; a record with bulk — a measurement sweep, a transcript — travels as an attachment while the body keeps the verdict. And knowledge already held in a durable document is pointed at, never copied: "the kitchen follows the county's hygiene plan — the posted plan carries the steps." The document stays the record; the fact says what it means for the project.

**Informs, never demands.** A fact sits off the project's attention surface: it drives no work, and nothing owes it a response. That is the line against the gap — "the group has twenty-two children registered for the autumn term" informs; set against a licence that allows twenty, it demands — and the two stay separate entries even when one produced the other, the fact holding the knowledge, the gap carrying the tension and pointing back at it. The same line runs inward: the moment the entry prescribes — this must be done, by then, by whom — a decision is being smuggled in a signal's clothes. Possible consequences and remedies may stand as unsettled dialogue material; committing to one may not. No automated check reads the prose for this: a wrong-kind fact is caught at drafting or not at all.

**Cited and extracted.** Later decisions ground in a fact: they take it as premise or cite it as empirical proof, which is why the claim must stand alone — it becomes the floor other entries reason from. And facts are extracted as often as written fresh: when a claim about the world surfaces inside another entry — an aside in a completed-work record, a finding mid-dialogue — carrying its own pull and no delivery of its own, it becomes its own fact, and the host keeps the mention, not the full story.

**Answering a question.** A fact may close a question by answering it, and it carries the question's context: the narrative restates the question's framing or names the unknown it resolves, so the reader does not have to reconstruct the connection. "Which provider should ship our parcels?" is not closed by "the courier ships for €4.50–5.80 per parcel" standing alone — a finding with no framing leaves the question open beside it. And the judgment is on the presence of that context, never on whether the answer is right.

**Corrected or retired, never fulfilled.** A fact has nothing intrinsic to resolve: no act completes it, and later entries never "address" it. When the world moved or the measurement improved, the corrected claim supersedes it. When it is no longer true or no longer relevant and no successor exists, it is closed by whatever entry carries the news that it stopped holding — any kind except a question, an actor, or an annotation, which state no findings — and what qualifies the closer is that it states what changed, since a retirement that merely restates the retired claim explains nothing. Closing where superseding was possible carries its own rationale — "not superseding: no updated measurement is available, just retiring the stale value" — so a later reader knows the missing successor was chosen, not overlooked.

**Enrolling in the pull index.** Some knowledge must be found before anyone knows to ask — how a cupping is run, how deliveries are logged: material to pull rather than guess at. A fact may enroll in the pull index for exactly that exposure, never on the drafter's say-so: every enrolled fact costs a line at the opening of every session, so the call is put to the user. The craft is the title, written as a retrieval cue — enough to recognize that pulling the fact will help: "how we run a cupping: sample sizes, scoring sheet, who calibrates" pulls, "cupping notes" does not. One primary topic groups it. And an enrolled fact pays its length at every pull: the more often it is read, the terser it earns being.

**Honesty in the small fields.** Confidence is proportionate to the evidence: high requires strong evidence, not confident phrasing. The layer names the scope of the claim, not its importance.
