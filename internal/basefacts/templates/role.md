# Granting a part — the role kind

A role records the grant of a part: one named participant takes on a shape of contribution to this project — this actor participates as this. It is a decision — something committed to, not something noticed — because a part is granted and relied on: from here on, the project routes work and dialogue as if it holds. Its reader arrives asking three things — whose part is this, what do they hold here, and when does something come to them — and a good role is the shortest record that answers all three.

**The actor comes first.** A role binds a participant the graph already knows: capture the actor, then the role. A role never introduces a person — it points at one — and it binds to the actor's stable name, not to any single entry, so a later refinement of the actor's record — a corrected spelling, a sharpened description — leaves every role standing exactly as written: an identity fix owes no successor roles. And when one conversation yields both halves — who someone is and what they do here — that is two entries drafted together, actor and role: deferring the role half is a stated choice, never a silent drop of what was volunteered.

**The part is granted in dialogue, not assigned.** A role commits two parties at once: the project, to routing work and dialogue this way from here on, and the named person, to carrying what the role says they hold. That is where its why comes from — the part is settled with the person, in dialogue, not decided about them: in a child-care group, "Marta holds the settling-in period for new children" records Marta taking the part on in the team's dialogue, not a rota drawn up in her absence. What was granted there is what the entry records; a part granted in the person's absence routes work to someone who never took it on.

**Shape enough to route by.** The substance of the entry is the participant's specialization and preferred contribution — the authority they hold, the decisions that are theirs, what is delegated away and what must come to them — stated clearly enough that another participant can tell when to bring work or dialogue to this person. In a coffee roastery, "Ana holds sourcing and the roast profiles: she selects and approves every green lot, sets profile changes, and purchases over the standing limit go through her — day-to-day roasting runs without her" can be routed against; "Ana contributes to the roastery" is a role in form only. Naming what runs without the person carries as much shape as naming what waits on them. And the shape belongs to one person: a role scopes exactly one named participant, and a participant may hold several roles at once when the parts genuinely differ.

**The weight carried here, not the background.** The recorded confusion between role and actor is expertise, so the boundary deserves stating outright: what a participant brings from outside — training, years in a trade, a qualification — is actor material; the weight that background carries in this project is the role. In a coffee roastery, "trained cupper, twenty years buying green coffee" describes the person; "every lot ships on her approval" describes the part. When drafting a role pulls toward biography, that sentence belongs in the actor entry, and the role keeps only the authority the background grounds.

**Standing, not frozen.** A role records the stable part — what this person usually holds, not what they happen to be doing in the current stretch — yet standing does not mean permanent. When the participation genuinely shifts — the scope widens or narrows, responsibilities move to other hands, the specialization grows into new territory — a refined role supersedes the old one, the same way any decision retires when it no longer matches the commitment it recorded.

**It reads whole on its own.** The prose introduces the bound name itself — in timber framing, "Jonas carries layout and joinery decisions on site; cut-list changes are his call" opens with Jonas — so the record makes sense read alone. The part is described in the project's own words, not picked from a menu of standard titles: there is no fixed vocabulary of roles, and inventing one adds classification without adding shape. And the body speaks about the person and the part, nothing else — rules about how entries link or validate belong to the machinery, not to a role's prose.

**The person's exit retires the parts.** A role's ending is mostly derived, and that is its distinguishing lifecycle property: when the bound actor's identity closes — the person leaves the project — every role bound to that name retires with it, and no separate closing entries are owed. A role can also be retired directly — the part withdrawn while the person stays — by any entry that says why the part stopped holding; the stated reasoning is what qualifies the closer, not its kind. That path is permitted but unusual, because the cascade is a derivation, not a prohibition: most endings are either the participation shifting, which is supersession, or the person going, which the actor's own closure already carries.

**The layer is process.** Whatever depth the person's own work reaches — a part spanning strategic direction included — the role lands at process, because it is about how this project works, who holds what, not about the work itself. Topic labels are rarely owed: the named participant and the part already carry the subject. And confidence may honestly sit below certain, because a role is an evolving commitment about participation, not a settled observation, and parts drift as projects do.
