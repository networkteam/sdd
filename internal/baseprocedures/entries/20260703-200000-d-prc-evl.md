The evaluate procedure closes the learning loop on completed work: judge it through both lenses, present the read to the user, and turn the findings they want to keep into signals against the evaluated work. Anchor on the done signal (or the closed commitment — its closing done and downstream arrive with the chains either way).

## unit: assess

The evaluated work and its surroundings, served in full:

{{.entryChains}}

**Two lenses — always both.** A pass on one is not a pass; reason about them together in `evaluation`:

- **Inner** — is it sound on its own terms: code, architecture, concepts. Read what the done signal claims against what its commitment demanded — acceptance criteria, augmenting directives in the chain, deviations it names or omits.
- **Outer** — does it work in use: an end-to-end or smoke check, and does it serve the user and the product. Did it meet the target? What surprised? What gaps remain?

**Widen before you judge.** Search the graph for what has happened since the work landed — signals it produced, regressions or surprises captured near it, neighbors that strain or confirm it. Record the searches and what they surfaced in `widenReport`; inspect promising results in full before they shape the evaluation. When the evidence needs running code — a smoke test, a build — that is host work; do it and fold the outcome into the outer lens.

Present the evaluation to the user in their language: what held, what didn't, what it taught.

## unit: junction

Each finding worth keeping becomes a signal refed back at the evaluated work — that is how the evaluation outlives this dialogue. Put it to the user: which findings should be recorded, and is there follow-up work to raise?

Relay their answer verbatim: **record** carries `selectedFindings` — their pick in their words; capture each as its own move (anchored on the evaluated entry, this instance as `parent`). **conclude** ends the evaluation with nothing worth recording — a clean pass is a valid outcome; say so plainly rather than inventing findings.
