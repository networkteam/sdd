SDD's vision and current state will be presented to new collaborators as a landscape: regions of connected ideas, each opening with why we want it and which aspiration it serves, shown through complementary views, with every element honestly marked by maturity — works today, decided and taking shape, or idea in dialogue. The immediate use is onboarding material for design- and product-minded collaborators from partner organizations; example content may come from the digital-agency world as a likely first-adopter setting, but must never limit the material to that world or to software work (20260730-101601-d-cpt-r99). The founding story (docs/story.md) stays the narrative entry point newcomers read first; this material is its structured companion, answering the newcomer-understanding gap (20260715-172002-s-cpt-i5a) and serving the non-developer-access aspiration (20260422-122523-d-stg-x0l).

The regions: (1) a shared context — the graph as the living picture of the project world every participant, human or agent, reads from and reasons with, keeping the why, derived rather than maintained, honest about open questions; connected graphs across organizations belong here; (2) thinking in dialogue — how the picture grows and gets used, from grounded drafting and playback to briefings and the evaluation of finished work; (3) everyone working together — people participating through the surfaces, agents as dialogue partners that also work in the background over the graph, and dialogue as the connecting element, including between people directly, with SDD preparing meetings and afterwards sorting captures out of the notes; (4) the world flowing in — outside input arriving as connected candidates that only dialogue promotes; (5) extending the process — procedures, rules, and facts living in the graph, with making a project's own business domain explicit through shared vocabulary and semantics as a further case, where a graph ontology is one rough idea; (6) surfaces — from CLI agents over the built engine foundation toward a hosted version for non-technical people, noting honestly that the technical foundation exists while the low-friction path is not finished.

The views: a collaboration view showing who works here and how it feels, animated by short role vignettes whose pattern visibly transcends the example; a work-lifecycle view framed by two motions — work that moves the graph (dialogue over gaps and questions, forming directives, making plans: everything bringing the picture closer to the project world) and work that moves the world (implementing, building, acting, talking, as the graph demands), with outcomes feeding back as signals — inside which the detailed path runs from a noticed gap through dialogue grounded in aspirations, standing directives, and the graph-carried process to directive, plan, implemented work, and evaluation, where partial implementations landing with done signals count as progress, outer evaluation returns new signals, and many small concurrent loops replace sprint-after-sprint cadence, contrasted against the old lifecycle of emails, meetings, documents, and tickets; and the landscape map itself with the maturity marks.

The material starts project-local and is meant to be lifted later, with more diverse roles and settings, into the planned documentation site (20260715-181235-d-tac-c7b), whose outlook already carries the matching knowledge-in-to-knowledge-out storyline. The full region-and-view synthesis with per-region graph reading lists is in the attached record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A landscape map exists showing the regions with maturity marks, readable in a few minutes without SDD-internal vocabulary.
- [ ] The collaboration view and the work-lifecycle view exist as presentable material, the lifecycle view carrying the two-motions frame, the concurrent-miniature-loops message, and the contrast with the email/meetings/documents/tickets lifecycle.
- [ ] Role vignettes show how working this way feels, using agency-world examples that each state the pattern beyond the example.
- [ ] Every region opens with its why and names the aspiration(s) it serves, and every element honestly carries its maturity mark.
- [ ] Christopher, the project's designer and principal developer, reviews the material before it is shared outside the project.
