Authoring a base fact for this repository follows rules learned kind by kind through review, and they hold as a standing guideline for every base fact still to be written or revised.

**What the title must name.** For a fact about an entry kind, the heading names what the kind is for, and specifically what distinguishes it from its siblings rather than what it shares with its type. Two retitles taught both halves: one kind was renamed because its title described the artifact instead of the purpose, and another because "committing" names what every decision kind does, so the title blurred the very line the document then spends a paragraph drawing. Get this right first, because the framing drives everything below it.

**One claim per paragraph, with the reasoning to argue from.** Each bold-led paragraph carries one claim and the reasoning behind it, never a field checklist — an agent that can only comply cannot push back in dialogue, which is the whole point of serving this knowledge. Terser is better and there is no target length: say what the subject demands and stop. Two paragraphs circling one theme is the failure to watch for; it is what sank the first attempt at one kind entirely and forced a rewrite.

**Every decision kind says where its why comes from, with its own angle.** Dialogue is the framework's central mechanism, and a fact about a decision kind that never mentions it is missing the point of the kind. Each angle must be genuinely its own rather than a retelling of a sibling's. Signal kinds may honestly carry none — an identity arrives from the world declared, and an entry that asserts nothing had no reasoning to reach — but a zero has to be earned rather than defaulted to.

**Re-derivability, instantiated per kind.** A later session must be able to reach an entry's claims again from its writing, attachments and references. What is irreplaceable differs by kind, and the fact says which: for one kind it is the observation from the world, for another the reasoning that made a connection, for another the provenance behind a claim.

**Mechanics render from the declaration that enforces them.** No fact hand-writes a rule the code checks. Where a rule is not yet exported, export it rather than paraphrase it — a paraphrase is a second source that drifts, and the drift is invisible until someone trusts the wrong one.

**Override posture is chosen, not defaulted, and open costs more than closed.** A base fact bound to the framework's own logic is closed to override: the semantics are the framework's rather than any project's, so a project copy would freeze stale truth against a version that has since moved, and the write path already refuses such a supersede and says why. Rendering content from declarations the code enforces is one indicator of that binding, not the whole of it. A fact that is entirely hand-written judgment a project might reasonably reword stays open. But open carries an obligation in return: when an open base fact changes substantially, the shipped version supersedes its own predecessor rather than being revised in place, so a project holding an override sees that a newer base version exists and resolves the conflict deliberately — without the chain, the project's override silently outranks changed content and nobody finds out. That obligation is not yet exercised: no open base fact has changed substantially, and shipping such a chain means the superseded version keeps shipping so the chain still resolves. Weigh that cost when choosing open.

**Consolidate, never invent.** Every claim traces to a source. A claim worth having but unsourced goes to the user as a ruling to make, never blended into the draft as though it were already settled.

**Plain language, because the words propagate.** The words these facts use are the words entries get written in: an agent imitates what is in front of it, so an ornamental phrase here becomes one in every entry drafted after reading it. Domain examples are wanted — they are what makes a discriminator testable outside software — but every example names its domain, from one small consistent set, because an unmarked example teaches its vocabulary along with its logic. Reserved kind names are never used in their ordinary sense.

**Point rather than restate.** What the type-system introduction owns is referenced, never re-taught, and discrimination between kinds belongs to the discrimination fact rather than to any kind's own document. Summary and general topic craft belong to the capture procedure; per-kind conventions on topics, confidence and layer belong in the kind's own fact, but only where the source is more than one specimen's passing gloss.

**The audience test governs all of it.** These facts ship to projects doing no software work, so nothing may assume one. And this repository's own history stays out, including the history of the guidance itself: the aspiration-versus-directive test was once decided on whether a completion criterion existed, that form was discredited because both kinds may lack one by design, and several older surfaces here still carry it. A shipped fact may warn that the criterion cannot decide that pair; it may not mention that this project once got it wrong or where the stale copies live.

**The working order.** Settle semantics in dialogue with the user first, then compress or rewrite, then register. Where a draft is structurally wrong rather than merely long, rewriting from the sourced research beat editing every time it was tried; where it was merely long, a fresh reader told to analyse what each paragraph serves before cutting anything, and to surface judgment calls rather than make them, produced better cuts than the author could.

This guidance is deliberately project-local: it is how the framework's shipped knowledge gets written, which only makes sense to someone working on this repository, so it never ships itself. Most of it is checkable at capture time, which makes it a natural candidate to migrate into the rule kind with advisory enforcement once that kind exists.
