Engine and application stay primitive-providing layers; procedures compose those primitives declaratively in their own entries.

**The split.** The workflow engine executes declared workflows and stays ignorant of what any procedure means. The application registry offers generic, procedure-agnostic capabilities: argument-taking injection queries, read-only template values, ops for side effects. The procedure entry declares the composition: which knowledge a step serves, when it repeats, which fact a selected kind points at.

**Why: extension.** A procedure entry is graph-resident, project-overridable, and revisable by supersession; Go code ships frozen in the binary. Composition kept in the entry stays reachable for every adopting project, and generic primitives serve procedures that do not exist yet.

**Why: legibility.** What a procedure does is readable from its declaration instead of reconstructed from host code.

**The test for new work.** Design a new serving capability as a primitive first; a capability shaped like a single procedure's needs is the signal to step back and find the primitive underneath.

**Bounded exception.** The write-side ops newEntry and writingGuide remain capture-shaped registry commands, because graph writes and their gates must live in code. The abilities-as-extension-point idea (20260814-171302-s-cpt-5zq) is where that boundary would be revisited.

**Rule migration.** Once the rule-system plan (20260622-084244-d-tac-eho) ships the rule kind, this directive is the candidate to become a rule, binding at capture instead of by reading.
