The unslop writing skill applies to all dialogue and graph-entry writing in this repository, adopted repo-locally as a trial, and the commitment is to enforce that application. The measure decided so far is the pointer in the project instructions; a pointer alone cannot guarantee application, and how reliably it holds is part of what the trial observes. The skill's framing was corrected at adoption: the goal is the tersest text that carries the meaning exactly and unambiguously, not the removal of AI tells for their own sake, and the two registers it serves differ. Dialogue keeps a colleague's voice; graph entries stay level-headed and objective, with exact terms, because loose wording reduces an entry's meaning and extra length dilutes its informational content. That register discipline applies the entry-craft criterion (20260810-121912-d-cpt-7zr) at the language level rather than restating it: the criterion governs what an entry carries, the skill governs the words that carry it.

The skill is the first content-level answer to the open register gap (20260610-000624-s-prc-42x): a plain-language discipline applied where the loop feeds itself, in dialogue prose and entry authoring, not only at output surfaces. Whether it breaks that loop is what the trial observes; the gap stays open until the evidence is in.

The skill ships nowhere yet. It lives as a repo-local agent skill file, outside the graph and outside the binary, and project instructions carry only a pointer to it, never a restatement, so the skill file stays the single wording. The audience test (20260730-101601-d-cpt-r99) holds it there: until the guidance proves itself in use we cannot tell which parts are framework knowledge and which are this project's taste. The pending follow-up is twofold: judge the trial and decide how the guidance reaches every SDD project, and settle the open question of how the session shell serves it directly, so it reaches agents on any host without agent-skill files, with a base fact (20260707-140544-d-cpt-dtv) as the candidate home.
