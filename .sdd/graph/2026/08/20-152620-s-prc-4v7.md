Every published SDD release whose hand-written notes used em dashes was rewritten to the new notes standard and republished, so the back catalogue now reads the way the standard requires rather than only the releases cut after it. The bodies were fetched, rewritten, and pushed back with `gh release edit`; release flags, assets, and tags were untouched, and `releases/latest` still resolves to the previous stable.

**This act closes nothing, and no commitment is missing.** The standard was settled and applied in a single session: Christopher asked for the back catalogue during the same dialogue that produced the standard (20260820-151056-s-prc-2f8), and the sweep ran immediately. So no plan, activity, or gap ever existed to point at, and writing one now purely to give this done a completion target would put ceremony in the graph rather than a commitment anyone made. A reader looking for the standing commitment behind this act should stop looking; the lineage ref is the whole story.

**How the rewriting was done.** Each em dash was replaced by judgment rather than substitution, because the right replacement varies: sometimes a comma, sometimes a colon, sometimes a sentence break, and in a few places the clause read better folded into the sentence before it. A mechanical swap would have produced grammatical debris in prose that outside readers see. Highlight bullets were reshaped to the standard's form, closing the bolded name on a period. Before publishing, each rewrite was compared against its original on bullet count, heading count, and the changelog link, to confirm that only wording had moved and no claim, item, or link had been dropped along the way. The only differences that comparison surfaced were the carriage returns GitHub stores in release bodies.

**Two releases were deliberately left as they were.** One already carried no em dashes. The other is v0.1.0, whose body is a raw auto-generated commit changelog rather than written notes: its em dashes sit inside historical commit subjects, and rewriting those would falsify the log. The distinction that decided it is authorship, not surface: prose this project wrote is ours to restyle, and a record of what someone typed at the time is not.

**Evidence.** Each rewritten body is readable on its own release page under https://github.com/networkteam/sdd/releases, and a final pass over every release confirmed no em dash remains outside v0.1.0. Nothing here touched the repository, so there is no commit to cite: the published pages are the artifact.
