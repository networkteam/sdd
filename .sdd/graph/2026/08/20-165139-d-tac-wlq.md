Every serve the engine pushes on its own at a step gets a hard size limit that holds no matter how large the graph grows, and everything the limit drops stays one pull away. The rule has two sides. Pushed content is bounded by construction. What the agent asks for itself (show, view, search, read_attachment) always arrives complete.

Three serves have already outgrown their hosts:

- The catch-up briefing measured ~50KB, and a harness with a ~10K-token response budget cut it in the middle (20260719-122103-s-tac-jom).
- The session-opening serve has grown past its own 25KB limit. The test that should catch this passes, because its fixture never fills the unbounded sections (20260818-133703-s-tac-ayj).
- In the dialogue that produced this plan, the engage step served one anchor's reference chains at 81,277 characters. That is past the Claude Code client's own result cap and took a file detour to read.

All three are automatic pushes. The receiving side cannot repair a cut serve; the middle is simply gone.

This plan commits the push+pull restructure recorded for catch-up (20260719-124754-s-cpt-s37) and widens it to every automatic serve: the opening serve, catch-up, and engage push a short lead, and the agent pulls detail on demand. The mechanics carry the bound. An injected entry list declares a maximum size and truncates at whole-entry boundaries, never mid-content, and a truncated list closes with the count of dropped entries plus the view expression that fetches them. Brief-rendered lines cut unusually long first sentences at a declared length. Because the pushed lead is a few small sections plus fixed instruction text, fixed per-section budgets are enough to keep the whole response under its limit; no dynamic redistribution is needed.

Not everything can move to the pulled side. Some pushed text has to arrive whole to work at all, like the thinking-posture principles served at session open; cut mid-sentence they stop being an instruction. The design dialogue decides what stays pushed in full and records why.

It lands in v0.17.0 on purpose. The transition decision (20260820-162456-d-tac-o2v) removes the fallback skills in v0.18.0, so the serving model has to prove itself in a release where `/sdd` still exists as the way out. An earlier draft staged this as two slices, containment on the existing serves first and the restructure second; we dropped the split because capping lanes the restructure then deletes is paying twice. If the redesign drags near release, crudely capping the old serves stays available as a release-time call.

Out of scope: limits on explicitly pulled reads, and per-perspective catch-up briefings, which stay open design work on 20260719-124754-s-cpt-s37.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] No automatic step serve of the base procedures exceeds its declared byte limit, on any graph, at any entry count or summary length.
- [ ] An injected entry list truncates at whole-entry boundaries and names what it dropped: the count plus the view expression that fetches the rest.
- [ ] One entry cannot break a limit: brief-rendered lines cut unusually long first sentences at a declared length.
- [ ] Explicit pulls (show, view, search, read_attachment) are never truncated by these limits.
- [ ] The opening serve, catch-up, and engage push a short lead with pull pointers instead of full expansions.
- [ ] The call on what stays pushed in full is recorded with its reasoning.
- [ ] A size test measures the full response against a realistically populated graph and fails when growth breaks a limit.
- [ ] A live session on a harness with a ~10K-token response budget runs opening, catch-up, and engage without one truncated serve.
