Kind knowledge lives in the graph as two facts per kind — one carrying semantics and craft for whoever drafts an entry, one carrying the checklist and calibration for whatever checks it — and the capture writing guide becomes a consumer of that pool instead of holding hand-written kind text of its own.

The pool exists already, aimed at one consumer: an indexed type-system overview plus an unindexed detail fact per kind, pointed at from each capture lane (20260726-193730-d-tac-zrh), all of it for the drafting agent. Adding the checking side closes the gap that the live guide judges kind-shaped drafts with no kind knowledge at all (20260811-183929-s-tac-fu8), and supplies what the capture-guide architecture left parked as future work — the kind-specific instructions it names as the guide's third context element (20260810-141555-d-cpt-20r). The guide's prompt renders both from the graph: the overview fact as the framework floor beneath every judgment, and the drafted kind's checking fact above it.

Two facts rather than one carrying both sections. Checking material is rule-shaped — it fires at a recognizable juncture, the drafted kind being this one, and it is checkable, which is the axis the rule design settled on — so as its own entry it becomes a rule by ordinary supersession once that kind ships (20260622-084244-d-tac-eho). Appending it to the authoring fact was considered and dropped for exactly that reason: migration would then mean surgery on an entry that also carries authoring prose. The accepted cost is that two homes can drift, which is why the checking fact cites the authoring fact for what a kind means and adds only what checking needs — the authority-not-wording boundary already fixed for the knowledge homes (20260730-110228-d-cpt-rh6). Calibration is the checking fact's own contribution with no counterpart on the authoring side: what not to flag is knowledge only a checker needs, and its absence is what produces the find-something bias that the same architecture splits capture checking in two to escape, one guide judging a draft in isolation and another judging it against the graph (20260810-141555-d-cpt-20r).

Sequencing follows from where the knowledge lives. Per-kind calibration of the guide waits on the facts rather than running ahead of them, which re-sequences the calibration path still open on the rollout directive (20260811-141038-d-tac-rvu): specimen sweeps resume once a kind's semantics are stated, because ground truth read off a stated semantics is worth more than ground truth invented specimen by specimen — the first sweep spent most of its length re-deriving what a done is even for. What does not wait is anything wrong under every architecture, among it that the guide is never shown which entries a draft closes or supersedes and therefore cannot tell what act the entry performs.
