An entry exists to make a later situation go better: pulled — by a human or an agent, into dialogue, implementation, or reasoning toward insight — into a moment its author never witnesses, it earns its place only by improving what happens next there. This guiding directive commits that purpose, the criterion a good entry meets, and a closed set of three failure axes — fit, duplication, conflation — each with its own systemic cost and its own repair.

## Purpose

An entry serves a reason, never the process. The graph carries data and instructions alike, so one discipline governs knowledge entries and instruction-bearing entries equally. The work that matters happens outside the graph; the entry's job is to carry the context that work needs so nobody rebuilds it. The test is never well-formedness but whether pulling the entry changes what someone does next — and because the pull may be interactive or automated, the entry must serve readers it will never see.

## The criterion

A good entry is about **one thing**; **minimal**, so that removing a sentence loses meaning; **standing alone**, so that a reader who was not in the dialogue misses nothing they need to act; written in **exact, right words**; and **shaped for the pull** — skimmable, with headings, emphasis, and paragraphing carrying weight as its kind requires. Its body holds **meaning that stays true**. A figure belongs in the body when it is the claim itself — a committed enumeration, a contracted limit; when a figure is evidence for a claim, the claim carries the sentence in its general form — a+b=c, never 1+2=3 — because evidence promoted into meaning gets restated as context for the next dialogue until the numbers crowd out the principle. Evidence scales its home: a small observation may sit inline, named as the dated evidence it is, while a record — tables, sweeps, transcripts — lives in an attachment, so the body keeps the verdict and never the bulk. The body stands alone without its attachment — verdicts and relationships in the body, the record behind them in the attachment, restating across that line only what the relationship needs. ## The three axes

These three are the whole instrument — spoilage is judged against them, and extending the set means superseding this directive. Their repairs are distinct actions: cut or write-in, point, split.

**Fit — words match meaning, in both directions.** Dilution is the same meaning carried by more sentences than it needs; its cost falls on every context the entry is co-pulled into and compounds with connectivity. Repair: cut. Stranding is the reverse — meaning alive in the dialogue that never reached the body, so the pull fails silently later. It is the one failure that becomes unrepairable: the other axes can be repaired from the graph afterwards, but what never reached the body is gone when the dialogue ends. Repair: write the missing meaning in at capture. An author cannot detect their own stranding — they fill the gap from context without noticing — so the test belongs to a fresh reader.

**Duplication — content another entry owns.** The graph is immutable, so every restatement multiplies the cost of future change: adjusting the owning entry then demands supersession or refinement everywhere a copy sits. Repair: point instead of restate. The discriminator: borrow words only to convey the **relationship** — why the target matters here — never the target's content. The relationship is local knowledge no other entry holds, so stating it duplicates nothing; a ref's desc does not discharge this duty, since desc serves ref inspection while the body must carry the relationship into its narrative. The same test runs backwards over ref selection: a ref whose relationship is not worth stating is mechanical and should not exist. It is one mechanism at every boundary — body to ref'd entry, body to its own attachment, served teaser to pullable fact — the authority-not-wording line the knowledge-homes directive fixes (20260730-110228-d-cpt-rh6).

**Conflation — content the entry is not about.** Selection is semantic: procedures and readers pull entries by kind and meaning, and an entry fusing two things can be pulled cleanly into neither situation it half-serves — the cost lands on machine selection as much as on reading. Repair: split into entries that reference each other. The calibration between ceremony and conflation is the purpose itself: content that is its **own concept** or carries its **own lifecycle** earns its own entry when the resulting constellation serves future pulls better — a split no future situation would pull separately is ceremony, and everything else stays in the body.
