Every step serve composes from named lanes, so that what varies in it serves separately from what does not. This is the commitment that retires the duplication constellation (20260811-164958-s-tac-9l8), whose three classes all reduce to one cause: the served-once memory has no boundary at which to dedup. What a step injects becomes a separately identified block rather than text woven into one instruction unit, and the memory then withholds the lanes that did not move (20260705-010751-d-tac-dbk).

Hashing harder cannot substitute. A content hash answers only identical or not; it cannot locate which part of a block differs, so on a joined unit one changed byte re-serves the whole. Finer granularity needs a boundary to hash *at*, and boundaries come from declaration, not from hashing — which is why the same repair on the shell's framing turned out to be declaration-only, needing nothing from the renderer (20260812-154551-s-tac-pp4). That record names the classes left over as needing render-level work; this is that work, carried to step serves.

What decided it now (20260825-234504-s-tac-jro): the classes were already recorded, and the measurement added the proportion. In a capture's opening serve the injected topic table is the *only* varying part — roughly a fifth of the serve holding the other four fifths out of deduplication — and the report schema repeats almost entire across every serve of a session. A small hostage-taker and a large hostage is what makes the split worth its cost.

Four things follow. **Inject calls gain a stable, readable identifier** the instruction prose can reference by name; today an inject carries only its function and arguments, and the function name does not distinguish two injects of the same function. **The schema becomes a lane, but an implicit one** — it is generated from the step's collect list and the procedure's declared state, never authored, so no spec declares it and its identity derives from procedure, step and schema variant. **Serve policy is per lane rather than global**, because stranding is not uniform: losing instruction prose fails loudly and the agent says so, while losing the topic labels fails silently — the agent mints a near-duplicate label, and labels are write-once with no alias mechanism, so the damage cannot be undone (20260601-162249-s-cpt-k1b). The topics lane therefore renders as bare labels and serves every time; at that size, repeating it costs less than the risk of withholding it. **Recovery stays the whole-position `fullReplay`** that exists today: naming makes a per-lane pull possible later, and this decision deliberately does not build it, since nothing yet shows the coarser escape failing.

Against the neighbouring commitment: the serve-size plan (20260820-165139-d-tac-wlq) bounds what any one response may weigh, and its mechanisms — truncating injected entry lists at whole-entry boundaries, cutting over-long brief lines — target content that grows with the adopting project's graph; its reasoning treats fixed instruction text as a constant to budget around rather than something to cut. A capture's opening serve is almost entirely that constant, and its problem is not weight on one response but repetition across many, which no size cap addresses without cutting the instruction itself. The two are complements rather than rivals — named lanes give that plan's pull pointers something to point at — and neither waits on the other.

Least settled is the per-lane policy: only the topics lane has been reasoned through, and what the default should be for lanes not yet examined is left to the work that builds this.
