A project's business domain has no explicit home in the graph: domain terms carry their meaning only implicitly, in prose spread across entries. SDD already demonstrates on itself that vocabulary with agreed meaning can be graph-resident, so the pattern exists and is simply not applied to a project's subject matter. Actual state, from a dedicated exploration sweep of this graph (2026-08-18, searching from several angles: ontology, glossary, domain vocabulary, shared semantics, domain modeling, term definitions): no entry treats a project's domain as explicit vocabulary, and no decided direction exists for it. Expected state, grounded in the process-side precedent: SDD's own working vocabulary — kinds, reference kinds, topics, actors, procedures — lives as graph-resident, supersedable knowledge (20260812-180000-s-prc-typ), where a term is defined once, pointed to instead of restated, and revised through the same dialogue that moves everything else; what SDD does for its process vocabulary it should be able to offer for a project's domain, because diverse participants — domain experts, designers, agents — reasoning over one project need terms with settled meaning.

Candidate direction, explicitly a candidate and not a commitment: a graph ontology — concept entries with typed relations over the domain — is one rough idea for the mechanism. The gap surfaced while shaping the vision-landscape plan (20260818-133112-d-tac-okx), which carries it as the exploratory edge of its extending-the-process region.
