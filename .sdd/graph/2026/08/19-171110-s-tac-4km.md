The engine's serving primitives do not follow supersession across repo boundaries: entryChains and entryHead pass a cross-repo ID through unwalked, and requireBody refuses it outright, so a graph-local procedure cannot serve or point at a connected repo's fact with live-head and fail-loud semantics.

**Actual.** Verified in code during the capture-time kind-knowledge delivery (20260818-203642-d-tac-vzz): workflowLiveHead returns cross-repo IDs unchanged and entryChains' requireBody errors on them — even though the engine's query context already composes dependency graphs and model.Graph.ResolveAcross prescribes owner-graph head-walks. No shipped procedure serves a foreign ID today, so nothing is broken live.

**Expected.** From the primitives directive (20260819-152855-d-cpt-x8m) and the driving scenario named in dialogue: a team authors custom procedures once in a shared repo, connects that repo into its other projects, and those procedures serve and point at the shared repo's facts. Cross-repo IDs are then a procedure's ordinary way to name its knowledge, and the primitives exist to serve exactly such procedures that do not exist yet.

**Proposed constraint, to be settled by the exploration.** An ID named by a procedure would resolve relative to the procedure's owning repo: a shared-repo procedure naming a companion fact by bare ID would mean its own graph, not the running project's — a hard-coded prefix looks unworkable, since each consuming project chooses its own alias for the connected repo.

**Candidate shapes, unweighed.** Walk foreign chains via ResolveAcross on the serving side, so the logged read and the dedup key stay the same head ID and member unavailability fails loud under requireBody — or keep serve-time cross-repo resolution deferred to write-time authority, as the refsResolve gate deliberately does. An exploration decides the shape; the connected-repo test fixture for the procedure suites is the main cost either way.
