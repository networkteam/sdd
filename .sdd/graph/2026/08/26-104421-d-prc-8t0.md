Widening a search means one query per hypothesis about how the sought entry would have been written, not several rephrasings of one guess.

The discipline is not new: the Widen move has prescribed searching from several angles since June (20260603-154135-s-prc-lud), and the multi-probe cost gap assumes agents already do it (20260702-204144-s-tac-j5a). Neither says what makes one angle different from another, and that is where it fails — an agent can issue three queries, satisfy "several", and have varied only the wording of a single guess. This entry's own drafting is the case: two hypotheses returned nothing relevant, and the third surfaced the whole prior art it now rests on, including a discipline shipped three months earlier.

An angle is a hypothesis about the entry being looked for: who would have written it, what prompted them, and whether they framed it as the situation they were in, the solution they chose, or the mechanism involved. Each hypothesis yields one query, and the queries go out together. Because retrieval matches on meaning rather than wording, rephrasing inside one hypothesis changes little — the hypotheses have to differ, not the words. A known exact term still earns a text or hybrid probe alongside; that is a different mode, not another angle.

The violation is asserting that the graph holds nothing on the strength of one guess. Absence is established only when hypotheses framed differently from each other come back empty.

One caveat on this directive's own shape: it is principle text, and principle text is exactly what has been observed to leave interpretation latitude that concrete steps tied to a specific moment remove (20260528-170354-s-prc-uq8). It states what must hold. The procedure instructions that carry it are where it has to become a step at the moment of searching, or it will read as agreeable and change nothing.
