Specimen-grounded per-kind guidance manufactures exactly the narrowing it is most exposed to: the only corpus available for grounding is this repository's own graph, where every done signal records software work and commits, branches and test suites are what evidence looks like — so guidance drawn from it quietly assumes its readers are building software, when what ships has to hold for projects doing none.

The mechanism is the sweep's own strength turned around. A sweep grounds each discriminator in contrast-selected specimens rather than intuition, which is what makes its output trustworthy — and is also what hands the corpus's composition authority over the wording (20260811-141038-d-tac-rvu). A discriminator lifted from a software specimen arrives already worded in software terms, and the narrowing is invisible from inside the sweep: the discriminator is genuinely well-grounded, the specimen genuinely supports it, and everyone judging it works on this repository. The audience test already names this failure mode and its asymmetry (20260730-101601-d-cpt-r99); what a sweep adds is a repeatable mechanism that produces it on evidence no one has reason to doubt.

The narrowing has already happened once, one specimen into the first sweep. A three-branch taxonomy of what different done signals owe their reader was drafted in dialogue, and one branch was worded "closes nothing, cites commits"; the correction was that commits are a special case of software development — SDD must hold for work on paper or wood. The general form had been in the graph the whole time: the durability rubric states evidence as appropriate to the act, with a commit for in-repo source changes, a URL, an attachment, or a participant's attestation where nothing changed (20260617-155740-d-prc-kqx). Availability was not enough to prevent the pull.

So the sweep method needs a step it does not have, between banking a discriminator and writing it into guide text: restate the discriminator as a demand the act makes — durable evidence appropriate to what was done — and audience-test that wording before it ships.
