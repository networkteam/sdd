Supersede resolution silently redefined in-degree from *how many entries point at me* to *how many references land on me*, and 20260705-130245-d-tac-cad has to state which one it means before its weighted in-degree ships. Until ranking resolved references to live heads (20260731-083858-s-tac-dmk), the two readings were the same number: an entry does not reference the same target twice, so counting references and counting referencing entries agreed. After resolution they diverge, because an entry that cites both a predecessor and its successor has both references land on the same head, and that single source is then counted twice.

The shape recurs rather than being accidental. Every instance the evaluation found in the live graph (20260810-121909-s-tac-a1g) is a signal describing a supersession — an entry that says "X was replaced by Y" naturally references both X and Y — so the pattern reproduces whenever the graph does the thing it is supposed to do. The gap that opened this whole line is itself one of them: 20260729-095323-s-cpt-tpb grounds in both the retired contract and its successor, and both now resolve to 20260730-171311-d-cpt-0cv, so tpb contributes twice to that entry's in-degree. Exact counts and the full census live in the evaluation's attached findings record; they are a dated snapshot of a moving graph and are not repeated here.

Nothing user-visible is wrong today, and this is not urgent. The inflation is a single extra count on a handful of entries, heat is affected in the same proportion and by the same cause, and coldness — which divides by in-degree — is nudged rather than distorted. What makes it worth recording is not the magnitude but that the behaviour is unstated: 20260729-111657-d-cpt-x6z specified that "in-degree stays a count, because a fractional count has no meaning" while resolution moved what was being counted underneath that sentence. The shipped semantics were inherited, not chosen.

Why this lands on the weighting plan rather than being fixed in passing. A weighted in-degree makes the question sharper, not softer: if one source entry contributes two weighted references to the same head, the coldness denominator compounds a single relationship, and the compounding is invisible in the resulting number. Three options are open — deduplicate by source entry when resolving, so in-degree counts entries as it used to; keep counting references and say so explicitly, accepting that a chain-spanning citation weighs more; or deduplicate only in the weighted form while the raw count stays literal. Each is defensible and they are not equivalent under the coldness lane. This is that plan's call to state deliberately, which is exactly the posture it already took toward the hop factor itself.
