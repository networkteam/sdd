A done signal's unit is one delivery event — a single act of work, with its evidence — so its conflation test runs on events and on content belonging to another kind, never on how many concepts the delivered work touches. Several unrelated changes verified and accepted in one landing are one event and belong in one done. Two acts, though, are two events: a meeting held, code committed, a version released are separate dones with separate evidence even when one working session produced all three. And whatever the event count, content that would stand as its own entry of another kind — a choice made, a problem noticed, a claim about how the world is, an open question, among others — belongs in that entry rather than inside the record of an act.

The live writing guide read the first of these the other way, and consistently. Run three times over a dense delivery record (20260810-145745-s-tac-sux), it returned substantive conflation twice on a branch-precedence change bundled into that landing, reasoning from the entry's own admission that the change belonged to a separate cleanup list — that a future reader chasing branch-precedence behaviour would have to pull an unrelated session-ending narrative to find it. A minor conflation on a retention-parsing fix followed the same reasoning. Dialogue judged both misfires, on the ground that a done is not the retrieval home for a concept: the deciding entry is. What a done is asked for is what happened, whether it can be trusted, and where to look further (20260811-160518-s-cpt-igk) — so the lifecycle that bounds it is the delivering act's, not each delivered concept's, and the criterion's own calibration applies with that reading (20260810-121912-d-cpt-7zr).

The same three runs missed the paragraph that genuinely conflates. One commit in that landing existed because a prior done's diagnosis of the store's state had been overturned, and the paragraph recording it asserts what the world is actually like — a claim with its own pull, no delivery of its own, and no home in a slice's delivery record. Not one run surfaced it, so the discriminator has to be written rather than left to general calibration.

Half of this rule already exists, scoped too narrowly. The legacy capture-time checker that the rollout directive narrows to graph-facing checks (20260811-141038-d-tac-rvu) forbids a smuggled decision, but only in a done that closes a gap, as a short-loop check. The kind-wide form covers every done, adds the fused-events direction, and generalizes the smuggled decision to any kind whose content rides along.
