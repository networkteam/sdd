A catch-up run served four of the five active focus decisions and cut the fifth, and the cut notice reported a byte ratio, giving the composing agent no way to tell that the dropped remainder was the current focus.

**Observed.** Claude ran the catch-up procedure (20260703-194500-d-prc-cat) on this repository's graph. Its compose step names the injected lanes as the entire input and forbids lookups until the briefing is delivered. The focus lane rendered four active focus decisions in ascending date order and ended with a notice of the form "kept N of M bytes — pull the rest: <view expression>". What fell past the cut was the newest of them, the engine-first transition focus (20260824-093559-d-tac-onl). The agent did not pull the remainder: the step forbade lookups, and the notice reported only how much was gone, nothing about what. The briefing named the engine-first arc anyway — two of its four threads hang from that focus — because the session-opening framing had served the same focus block uncut and that copy was still in context. From the lanes alone, the briefing would have called an August 11 focus current and dropped the arc entirely. The three other cut lanes and the framing's own cut carried notices of the same byte-ratio form. The omission came to light only when the user asked afterwards whether a good catch-up was possible from the cut lanes, and the two copies of the focus block were compared.

**Expected.** The bounded-serves plan (20260829-173342-d-tac-rzi) commits cuts that name what they dropped and how to pull it, and its third acceptance criterion requires every cut of an entry list to name the dropped count alongside a ready-to-run pull. Its closing done (20260831-130902-s-tac-ndd) recorded every criterion as met, earlier the same day as this run.

**The deviation.** The notice states the size of what was dropped, not its identity, so the reader cannot judge whether the remainder is worth retrieving. Both directions of the pull decision stay uninformed — no ground to pull, and no ground to skip. The decision is the one that matters for the bound: an agent that re-pulls every cut on principle spends the saving back.

**Reasoning, not observation.** The teaser contract (20260730-110228-d-cpt-rh6) names this failure on a different surface — a pointer that does not convey what reaching for it would buy works like no pointer — and its own grounding evidence has the same structure, an agent needing the knowledge before it could know it needed the fact. That contract governs the three knowledge homes and not cut notices, so the resemblance is a candidate reading rather than a rule already in force. A second candidate, unsettled: the focus lane renders in date order under a byte cap, which puts the newest focus exactly where the cut falls; whether that ordering is a separate deviation or part of this one is unresolved.
