Entries must distinguish requirements about who acts from changeable work assignments, so a change of hands alone creates no contradiction with the agreed work.

Embedding a current assignee in an otherwise independent observation or decision makes later execution by someone else appear to violate the decision or invalidate the graph. The substantive agreement may still hold unchanged. Keeping those meanings separate applies the entry-craft requirement that meaning stays true (20260810-121912-d-cpt-7zr) and supports autonomous action within shared direction (20260422-123814-d-stg-7lu).

The test is whether changing the person preserves the intended outcome and constraints. If it does, the assignment is not part of the observation or commitment. If it does not, the entry states the necessary person, qualification, or relationship and why it matters. For example, learning by a particular participant or obtaining that participant's first-use feedback requires their involvement; qualified sign-off requires the relevant qualification, without necessarily requiring one named person. These illustrate the test rather than exhaust the cases where who acts matters.

The involvement recorded by a focus (20260818-110300-s-prc-foc) does not make its actors exclusive executors or prescribe who must perform every step. Work may pass through different hands while everyone stays aligned on what should be done. A handoff alone neither requires a focus update nor justifies declaring a contradiction; it matters when it changes the agreed outcome or constraints.

Historical attribution remains meaningful: participants preserve who contributed the work and reasoning so later dialogue can return to them (20260911-125913-d-cpt-gso). That attribution does not assign subsequent work.
