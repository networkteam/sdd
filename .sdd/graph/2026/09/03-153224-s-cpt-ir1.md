When a principal works in project A and one of A's declared dependencies is not reachable for them, should the work require that access, or degrade per reference? Reachable means the composition can resolve the dependency for this principal: hosted, that they are a member of it; locally, that its cache exists, which a second-level dependency of a dependency may lack.

The demand arose while settling the dependency closure in 20260903-152247-d-cpt-yjc: the closure is computed from the configurations the composition can reach, which leaves open what happens to A's own reads and captures when part of A's horizon is missing. Today a read composes A's view through each declared dependency independently, and a dependency the resolver refuses fails the read as unavailable, with unauthorized and unknown indistinguishable to the caller by design (20260712-104530-d-tac-1mb). So the current behavior is the strict answer, reached by construction rather than by choice.

Strands that stand or fall together: whether a read in A should fail because B is missing, or succeed with B's references rendered as unresolvable and the reason named; whether a capture in A that references B should block only on the references that cannot resolve, which the capture-time rule that every reference must resolve or the capture is blocked already does, or refuse the capture outright; whether membership in A should imply read access to A's dependencies as a composition policy, or SDD should surface the missing access as a finding and leave the policy to the composition; and locally, whether an uncached second-level dependency is simply unreachable or fetched on demand.

Candidates, unsettled: require access, keeping today's behavior and making the expectation explicit that working in A needs A's horizon; or degrade per reference, so a person can read and ask about A with a partial horizon and captures are the only place the missing dependency bites. The second matches the wish voiced in dialogue, that not being able to clone a dependency should not stop reading and asking about it, with captures more limited because references into it cannot be followed. What could settle it is a decision after trying the partial-horizon read on a real connected graph, since the user-facing cost of each answer is what is unknown.
