Capture can turn changeable work assignments into entry substance, causing later changes of hands to appear inconsistent with decisions whose intended outcome and constraints still hold.

Christopher reported recurring behavior and supplied a session excerpt for review. An agent appended a named implementer to a directive without explaining why that person was necessary. The user removed the assignment. Later, the agent compared a new division of work with a historical decision's allocation, despite recognizing that the substantive coordination constraint remained satisfied. The user again had to distinguish changed involvement from changed agreement.

The governing expectation is now recorded in 20260911-131400-d-cpt-xhi. Capture and later reasoning should preserve substantive actor requirements while treating a handoff alone as no contradiction. The observed behavior instead creates avoidable reconciliation work and makes a valid decision appear stale or disobeyed.

The focus authoring fact already separates engagement from target content (20260818-110300-s-prc-foc), but this did not prevent assignment leakage when drafting another kind. Merely relocating names to focus entries also leaves the interpretation problem: a focus can be read too rigidly when work passes through different hands.

Entry-crafting base facts and the writing guide's knowledge need alignment with the governing distinction, including its application to focus involvement. These are the drafting and checking consumers of kind knowledge established by 20260811-184407-d-cpt-x7a. The supplied excerpt establishes the behavior, not which instruction or checker caused it; the distribution of guidance and the implementation remedy remain open.
