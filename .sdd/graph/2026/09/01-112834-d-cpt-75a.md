A summary exists so a reader — human or agent — can orient and decide without opening the entry: it is a pointer to the body, never a parallel record, and what it must be derives from the uses it serves, not from a fixed format.

The uses, and what each demands. Brief listings — view lists, search results, and the sections the engine serves into sessions and briefings — render the first sentence, often hard-truncated, as the entry's entire representation, so the first sentence alone must state what the entry observes, commits to, or did, leading with its substance. Chains and neighbor views use the summary as the stand-in for an entry not yet opened; the refs already carry their own per-ref descriptions, so the remaining sentences do not enumerate edges — they describe how the entry fits among its surrounding entries, with entry IDs as handles where they carry that fit, and nothing transitive. Every surface uses the summary to decide whether to open the entry, so it asserts nothing the body does not state — no invented actors, no graph-positioning commentary, no corrected identifiers — and owes no completeness either: it preserves the main meaning and may say what the entry will tell when read in full; a pointer earns the pull, it does not replace it. Briefings and LLM contexts consume summaries in bulk as token-bounded stand-ins, so terseness stands throughout, the configured graph language always governs, and the writing holds to the highest standard — concrete, exact words; no jargon, no metaphor, no filler — because summaries are what future sessions read first and their wording propagates: an agent that reads one vague formulation adopts it and spreads it, an effect observed repeatedly. The exact words are ideally the project's own — the open domain-vocabulary gap (20260818-143339-s-cpt-8h2) connects here: once a graph declares its vocabulary, a summary, like any entry, becomes gradeable against it; noted as a connection, not committed.

The first sentence is the load-bearing half, and it compresses by selection, never by stuffing: it carries the entry's single most important meaning in the shortest sentence that carries it — the shorter the better; everything else belongs to the remaining sentences or stays in the body.

Size is bounded by ceilings, never targets: at most three sentences, and no numeric length target anywhere in the instruction — a stated target is a goal generation stretches toward, observed repeatedly in the provider evaluation (20260901-093840-s-tac-ahc), whose judged runs found lede-stuffing the dominant summarize failure across every candidate model. Ceilings cap; minimization governs below the cap.

Consumers derive from this commitment rather than defining their own: the summary prompt template renders it, the summarize eval's judge tests it, and prompt-tuning rounds verify changes against the judged suites across candidate models. It builds on the April framing (20260414-152337-d-tac-h93), decided when summaries existed to replace recursive expansion — before brief listings and lanes made the first sentence the entry's public face — and instantiates for summaries the first-sentence and minimality demands the entry-craft criterion commits for bodies (20260810-121912-d-cpt-7zr). It answers the define-the-summary-scope question the drift catalog (20260427-151153-s-cpt-tdp) left for later dialogue — the scope is the entry plus its direct relationships, exactly as the uses require — while that catalog's post-generation repair paths stay open. And it closes the short-form proposal (20260517-180646-s-cpt-c43): the minimized first sentence is the compact identity form dense surfaces need, and a second generated field would be a parallel record of the same meaning with its own drift surface — rejected for the same reason the summary itself must not restate the body.
