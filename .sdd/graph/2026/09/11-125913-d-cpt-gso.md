SDD preserves an entry's participants so later work can return to the people and agents who did the work and reasoning, and reopen dialogue with them when an observation or decision is questioned.

The graph is an incomplete and possibly outdated picture of the project, as the working principles establish (20260810-190000-s-prc-way). The original participants may hold context, assumptions, and reasoning that the entry did not preserve. Revisiting a decision should involve those who took it: their involvement helps recover that context and maintains a culture of reasoning with people when reconsidering their decisions. This applies the dialogue-first aspiration (20260422-122136-d-stg-beb) to renewed dialogue, including a new meeting when the record alone cannot resolve the question.

Participants therefore follow the substance recorded in each entry, including observations and decisions developed before the capture session. When one person processes a meeting transcript, each resulting entry retains the participants in the work and reasoning it records. The person performing the capture does not replace them. Attending a meeting does not make someone a participant in every resulting entry. Capture must present this attribution for correction before confirmation, so the record preserves the route back to the relevant participants.
