The capture writing guide attributed text to a draft that the draft never contained: on a live capture, a substantive finding quoted the closure target's summary verbatim as if it were the draft's opening sentence. It happened while capturing the engine-recording verification done (20260831-200636-s-tac-eav): the guide returned a `form` finding whose quote is, word for word, the first summary sentence of the entry being closed — text appearing nowhere in the draft — and whose reasoning judges that sentence's tense as "the first sentence" of the entry, while the draft's actual opening states the opposite. The run used ollama glm-5.3-flash:cloud at think=high; the finding, the submitted draft, and the rendered prompt shape are preserved in the attached [reproduction record](./01-220355-s-tac-owp/writing-guide-quote-misattribution.md).

The expectation is the guide's own output contract: its prompt instructs that a finding's quote carries the draft's words verbatim, and the guide's scope is the draft in isolation. A finding quoting text the draft does not contain breaks that contract and puts a phantom issue in front of the drafter — here it cost one dismiss-and-name cycle at playback, and unnoticed it would have cut a correct paragraph.

Code reading establishes the surrounding mechanics without settling the cause: the closure target's summary is rendered inside the single draft block the guide is told to judge, above the body (`internal/llmops/writingguide.go`); the one-summary-sentence bound delivered a ~60-word paragraph, because `FirstSummarySentence` cuts at the first sentence boundary and this target's summary is one colon-structured sentence; and quote provenance is never checked — the parser accepts any string. That this rendering shape caused the misread is one candidate explanation; a model-specific weakness under the same layout is another. Both are unsettled and both are testable.

The reproduction-and-fix vehicle is the writing-guide eval suite (`internal/llmops/writingguide_eval_test.go`, eval build tag): a pinned case — a done draft closing a target whose first summary sentence is long and states the pre-act problem in present tense — reproduces the shape, and a suite-wide assertion that every finding's quote occurs verbatim in the draft's own fields makes provenance mechanical per model and guards whatever repair lands. Repair candidates, unsettled: restructure the prompt so the draft to judge and the reference material are plainly separate — today the closure edges sit inside the one draft block, and nothing says "judge this, the rest is reference only"; or drop or flag out-of-draft quotes after parsing — either under the standing constraint that closure edges stay visible to the guide, which misreads correct retirement dones without them (20260811-183929-s-tac-fu8).
