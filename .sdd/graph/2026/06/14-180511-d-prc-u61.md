When a directive carrying active `refines` refinements is superseded rather than closed, those refinements go stale. The refines contract (d-cpt-s9j) ties a refinement's closure to its target's *done signal*, so supersession — a different retirement path — leaves refinements dangling-active against a retired target; and because entries are immutable, a refinement cannot be silently re-pointed to the successor. This is the graph-structure instance of the untraced-dependent-surface gap (s-prc-guf): a superseded directive's refinements are a connected surface left stale. Two viable resolutions, both deliberate and dialogued: (1) fold the refinement's content into the successor and close the refinement — preferred at supersede time, and it shrinks the graph; (2) supersede the stale refinement with a fresh one re-anchored on the successor (or promoted to a standalone directive when the successor no longer covers the refined area) — the recovery path when the fold-in was missed, or when the refinement warrants staying its own entry. What's rejected is doing either automatically: an auto-sweep closes refinements that may still be valid, and an auto-repoint mis-anchors when the successor's scope has shifted — the choice is an authoring judgment. The skill should flag the stale refinement at supersede time; `expand(refs(inactive))` already detects the symptom. A candidate to become a graph-resident rule once the rule system (d-cpt-30v) lands.
