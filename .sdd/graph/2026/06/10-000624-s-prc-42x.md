Agent language register remains jargony and overly complex despite repeated per-surface fixes, and the pattern suggests a structural cause: the graph itself. Prior work disciplined SDD-vocabulary leakage surface by surface — the three-surface register rule (d-prc-qse), its session-opener patch after the rule alone still leaked (s-prc-qj6), and the catch-up briefing tone blocks (d-tac-k4l) — yet dialogue prose, entry authoring, and generated summaries have no plain-language discipline at all. Christopher observed further instances that were never captured, so the graph understates the frequency.

The mechanism hypothesis: dense entries feed the register back. Every catch-up, playback, and entry load injects academic, abstraction-heavy prose into the agent's context, biasing subsequent turns toward the same register — a self-reinforcing loop that per-surface output instructions cannot break, consistent with the text-instructions-unreliable insight (s-stg-3vr). Candidate directions: plain-language discipline for entry authoring and summary generation (the densest feedback source), a swap-list mechanism at draft time mirroring the briefing tone blocks, and treating register as a property of graph content, not only of output surfaces.
