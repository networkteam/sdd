Make pre-flight calibration adjustable — including per project — by turning pre-flight into a second consumer of the graph-resident rule corpus, rather than leaving rubric and severity calibration baked into bundled skill and code. This is the committed response to the gap that pre-flight calibration is cumbersome to tune and impossible for downstream consumers (s-cpt-bnj).

Today calibration lives only in the bundled base; a project that merely uses SDD has no lever to tune pre-flight to its own work short of forking. Once rules are graph-resident (d-cpt-30v), calibration guidance can live in rules — binding rules pre-flight enforces, advisory rules whose calibration guidance it factors into severity — so a project layers local calibration depth onto the bundled base by capturing local rules, no fork required. Pre-flight becomes the corpus's second consumer alongside the in-mode discovery move.

Chosen over the status quo of base-only calibration (the gap's named dead end) and over a bespoke per-project config format (a parallel mechanism duplicating what the rule corpus already is).

Gated on the rule system landing (d-cpt-30v); mechanism deferred to a follow-up plan. The central open question surfaced in dialogue: pre-flight is a mechanical consumer — it assembles validation context deterministically, not via the semantic sift the in-mode discovery move uses — so targeting the calibration rules applicable to a given (entry kind, check) almost certainly wants a machine-checkable discriminator on the rule (a structured applies-to / trigger), distinct from the free-text "when to apply" that discovery matches semantically. That pulls forward the structural-trigger mechanism d-cpt-30v deferred, and likely eases the byte-stable-prefix caching concern (s-tac-osb) by making the injected set scoped and stable rather than load-everything. The rule build plan owns the trigger-field schema decision; this directive records that pre-flight-as-consumer is the requirement driving it. Also open: whether calibration is binding-enforced, advisory-weighted, or both.
