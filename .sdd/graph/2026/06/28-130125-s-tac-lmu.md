`sdd lint` has no defense-in-depth check for a `settled` directive that carries a closing edge. Creating one is blocked at capture — `validateCloses` rejects closing a settled directive — but a direct file edit, a concurrent-write race, or a validator bypass could leave a settled directive with an incoming `closes` edge on disk. Because `DerivedStatus` checks `settled` before `closed-by`, the anomaly then renders as `{status: settled}`, masking the rogue close rather than flagging it. A lint check that flags any settled directive with an incoming closing edge would surface the violation, mirroring the existing orphan-role and actor-invariant lint checks (defense-in-depth against direct edits and bypass). Surfaced while evaluating the shipped intent attribute (s-tac-o30).
