Ref-kind sharpness findings are evidence-gated: a kind-vs-body mismatch may be flagged at medium only when the observation quotes the body phrase that names a different admissible kind; with no quotable anchor the ceiling is low. Body-derived kind selection carries forward unchanged from the superseded calibration directive — write the body first, then tag the strongest relationship it states, with `related` as a gated last resort.

This replaces the superseded directive's never-medium rule for kind questions. That rule was calibrated against a validator that also judged applicability, where ungated mediums had measured zero real errors and recommended kinds the vocabulary forbids. Both conditions changed with the reliability plan (d-tac-tph): applicability is now decided by a mechanical matrix that blocks inapplicable kinds deterministically and injects the admissible set into the validator's prompt, so suggestions are constrained to admissible kinds — and a low-only ceiling had made the advisory ignorable in practice, removing the one moment a kind fix is cheap. The evidence gate keeps the medium tier falsifiable: the cited excerpt either supports the suggested kind or the finding is miscalibrated, which the pinned advisory-precision eval cases measure. If post-matrix mediums regress to noise, demotion is one supersession away with the eval corpus as proof. Severity for kind questions never reaches high on the LLM side — high remains reserved for desc-vs-body contradiction, with precondition violations handled mechanically before the validator runs.
