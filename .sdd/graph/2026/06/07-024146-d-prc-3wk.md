Commit to interactivity as the standard for agent–human dialogue in the sdd skill and any agent: handbacks must build understanding and require the user to produce, not skim-and-confirm, and the agent must surface its own assumptions for correction — so the alignment check runs in both directions, not just agent-checks-user. This sharpens the alignment-first architecture (d-prc-nkw), whose skimmable-output discipline is necessary but doesn't enforce engagement. One mechanic is already proven and we lean on it: d-prc-nkw's "pair abstraction with concrete examples" — carefully chosen, interesting cases — because a concrete example is what lets a reader's instinct catch a plausible-but-wrong framing, where abstraction slides by. The rest of the "how" stays deliberately open — which production modes, and how to scale tool use to the risk of misalignment (hard, since small assumptions slip in and the serious-misalignment detector is still human instinct) — to develop through practice and the teaching lens, not specced now.
