The ref-kind forward class is capture-order-bound, and `surfaces` — unlike `depends-on` — has no inverse, so a relationship discovered *after* its surfacer was captured cannot be recorded cleanly. Forward kinds live on whichever entry is written second; `depends-on`/`required-by` is therefore order-independent (either side can carry the edge), but `surfaces` never got its pair. In the common order — the surfacing work captured first, the thing it raised captured second — the surfaced entry must point back, and every admissible backward kind degrades the meaning: `grounded-in` and `builds-on` understate "you spawned me," and `addresses` is mechanically blocked when the surfacer is a terminal done. The harm is twofold — a lossy kind, and worse, a mis-targeted ref: with no kind that says "raised by," authors re-anchor the edge onto a more convenient live decision where a backward kind at least applies, which is exactly where capture-time kind disputes are then born. This contests the completeness principle in (d-cpt-s9j), which dismissed a `surfaces`-inverse as already covered by `grounded-in`/`addresses`, and repeats the shape of the directionality gap (s-cpt-vxb) that earned `required-by` as the first paired inverse. Proposes `surfaced-by` as the symmetric inverse — the surfaced entry points at the work that spawned it, cleanly and queryably ("what did this entry surface?") — with documentation-only (pin `grounded-in`, carry the nuance in `desc`) as the lighter alternative; choosing between them is a downstream decision weighed against the growth cost of another kind.
