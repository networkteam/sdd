Catch-up answers two different questions, and heat serves only one — the other is heat's inverse. A returning reader is owed both "what is the project converging on" (structural center) and "what have I committed to but not yet acted on" (open loops). Heat, a sum over incoming refs, answers the first and is structurally blind to the second: an unacted commitment has by definition no incoming refs. They are one axis at opposite ends — an entry is born cold (no refs, pure open loop) and migrates toward hot as the graph acts on it, so the single inbound ref that lifts a plan into the hot lane is the exact event that should demote it out of the open-loop lane; the lanes hand off on the first ref. Recency was only a proxy for unacted-on — the real signal is the absence of incoming refs, decayed slowly by the entry's own age so ancient orphans fade rather than nag (SDD is not a todo list). Proposed mechanism: a new `sdd view` rank algorithm, coldness(e) = decay(entry-age) / (1 + in-degree(e)) — the existing decay vocabulary applied to entry age instead of ref age — surfaced as a second catch-up lane, with fresh entries woven into the narrative through their own upstream refs (a genuinely disconnected fresh entry signals a new line of work and earns its own thread). A third "delta" lane — what changed since I was last here, via a seen-watermark — is a distinct concern, deferred. See [design note](./25-115201-s-cpt-6h6/design.md) for the full reasoning, the coldness formula, and the deferred lane.
