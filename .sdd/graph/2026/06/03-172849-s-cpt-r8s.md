Capturing a decision that is settled at the moment of capture — needing no follow-up and no later closing done signal — has no clean path: the lifecycle model treats every decision as active until something closes it, so a born-done decision either lingers misleadingly in "active decisions" or needs a ceremonial companion done signal. This is the recurrence that the closed no-action-closure gap (s-prc-k6r) asked us to watch for: that gap was resolved by routing no-action decisions through the grooming sub-skill (d-prc-oka) as a later closure pathway, but grooming is after-the-fact cleanup — it does not let a decision be born closed at capture. The pattern now shows up in three places: dismissing intake noise (sidestepped today by a terminal done signal in the intake model d-cpt-fbi), a ruling that declines with no work ("we won't add passwords"), and especially retroactive seeding when adopting SDD on an existing project, where already-enacted decisions are all born-done and a companion done signal for each is pure ceremony — tying this to the low-friction adoption gap (s-stg-qg0). The open question, deferred by s-prc-k6r and now warranted by recurrence, is whether to add a first-class settled-at-capture (born-closed) decision mechanism, and what form it takes — a self-closing flag, a terminal decision state, or simply a sanctioned capture-time pairing with a done signal.
