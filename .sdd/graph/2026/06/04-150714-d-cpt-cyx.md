Directives carry a stored `intent` attribute with three values — `pending`, `guiding`, `settled` — written at capture and immutable, that mechanically distinguishes a directive's role so tools, status, and catch-up needn't re-interpret the body each time. `pending` is action-demanding (active, awaiting the work that a done signal will close); `guiding` is standing direction that shapes other decisions without itself demanding a build (active, retired only by supersession — the soft-standing role d-stg-574 chose over a hard contract); `settled` is terminal at capture — a decision complete on arrival with no follow-up, its rationale carried in its own body. This resolves the born-done decision gap (s-cpt-r8s): rather than storing a closed status (a second source of truth) or pairing every settled decision with a done signal (an extra entry), we store only the non-derivable input — intent — and let DerivedStatus compute terminality from it (intent == settled → terminal), keeping status a single derived function. The same attribute clears the conflation it surfaced from: status and catch-up can split pending directives (act on these) from guiding ones (standing context) mechanically. Status stays edge-derived for pending and guiding (a close or supersede edge moves them as today); settled needs no edge and renders as its own {status: settled}, distinct from {status: closed-by X} so a reader sees born-settled versus closed-by-later-work. Terminality now has two sources the derivation reads — kind == done (signals) and intent == settled (directives). Backward compatibility is read-time: an existing directive with no intent reads as unspecified — rendered exactly as today (plain active), never coerced to pending/guiding/settled, matching how the graph already treats absent stored attributes like confidence; the distinction is opt-in, and backfilling a known guiding directive (d-stg-574) is a deliberate supersession, not a blanket default. Chosen against alternatives weighed in dialogue: a stored closed-status (rejected — a second source of truth for a derivable fact), a paired decision-plus-done-signal (rejected — an extra entry per settled decision, painful at retroactive-seeding scale), and a new terminal decision kind (rejected — duplicates what intent expresses). Scope is the directive kind (activity and plan are inherently pending; contract and aspiration inherently standing); whether settled extends to activity/plan for seeding completed work is left open. This directive sets the shape; a follow-up type-system plan adds the frontmatter and the capture-time default for new directives, the derivation branch, the {status: settled} rendering and the pending/guiding split in status sections, and a pre-flight rubric requiring a settled directive to justify why in its body.
