Pre-flight calibration is caught in a non-converging patch loop, and the evidence now points at structure rather than rubric text. Each calibration round shipped a real fix — confidence-scored verdicts, the eight-kind ref vocabulary with a pinned calibration eval, most recently the applicable-never-high recalibration (d-prc-v0h) realized on June 5 (s-prc-lsg) — yet within three days new leak instances arrived (s-prc-lbv, s-prc-pex), and a verbatim transcript from June 9 (s-prc-l2d) shows both leak axes in one capture, including a `[high]` finding that withdrew itself in prose while still blocking. The accumulated instance gaps since April (s-prc-vvd and successors) document the cost: validation that oscillates trains participants to bypass it — "fighting the system" — eroding the trust the validator exists to provide.

The synthesis: rubric wording is not the bottleneck; the finding-generation structure is. Severity is committed before the rationale concludes, so a finding can talk itself out of existence and block anyway. Candidate structural moves, in priority order: (1) verdict-after-reasoning ordering — restructure finding output so severity is emitted only after the rationale, making self-retracting findings structurally impossible; (2) a mechanical post-parse guard that drops findings whose prose withdraws them, as defense in depth; (3) eval-corpus discipline — every captured leak instance becomes a pinned calibration case before any rubric change ships, turning live captures from the discovery surface into the regression surface. A rework should also account for calibration being unreachable for downstream consumers (s-cpt-bnj).
