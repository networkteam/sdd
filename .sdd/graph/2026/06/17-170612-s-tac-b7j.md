Across the two-runtime bake-off (20260614-133644-d-tac-dcn), both spikes landed at the same operation granularity — the Mastra spike exposes the sdd CLI as 1:1 tools, the Go spike (20260614-134017-d-tac-vvo) wraps the same finders/handlers in-process — and that parity is what makes the head-to-head valid: the score reflects runtime, bridge, and UI, not tool-surface design. The lesson is that a runtime evaluation must keep the agent plus its rendered instructions as the subject under test. The shared bar is outcome-framed ("a catch-up", "capture one entry"), which invites coarse catch-up/capture tools that move the loop into the bridge and hollow out two scored dimensions at once — SDD-loop fidelity (the bridge would do the loop, not the agent) and single-source instructions (20260614-110729-d-cpt-chi) (the rendered instructions would stop driving anything). Mirroring the CLI's granularity keeps both honest; worth stating in the rubric as a comparison-validity requirement, not a stylistic preference.
