Capture-time does not prevent supersede forks. The fork lint (now live-forks-only per d-cpt-rgx) catches forks after they exist, but nothing stops an author from creating one: superseding an entry that already has an active successor produces two competing heads. d-cpt-rgx settled the prevention rule — supersede the live head, not an already-superseded entry — but recorded it as practice with no mechanical enforcement. Close this with a mechanical capture-time check: when a new entry's supersedes targets an entry whose chain already resolves to a different, still-active head, the author is superseding a non-head and should target that head instead. Because SDD's mechanical pre-flight checks are binary (high-severity or absent), this enforces the rule as a capture-time block rather than an advisory nudge; a settled branch (the existing successor closed) is not a fork and is left alone.
