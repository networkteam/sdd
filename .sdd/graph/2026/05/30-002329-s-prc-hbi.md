Observed once: `s-cpt-kfl` was closed when the underlying capability (`expand(refs)`) shipped, but the gap was framed around a reader-facing symptom — blur in catch-up — that the capability alone didn't remove. At close time the reader-facing part was still undone. A follow-up finished it later, but only because the early close got noticed.

Why it's worth watching: closing a signal or directive when nothing has fulfilled it in whole is a quiet drop. Once an entry closes it leaves the open set, so any part of its framing that went uncovered is no longer tracked anywhere — that remaining scope is lost instead of carried forward, and nothing flags that the close covered only part of what the entry asked for.

This holds whether an entry closes because it was implemented or because it was dialogued as retired: the close is the last moment its whole framing is in view, so that's what the close should be checked against before it drops off the open set.

Low confidence — one observation so far. Whether this wants a closing-time check, a nudge, or just discipline is for a follow-up to decide.
