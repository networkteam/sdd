Refines the chunker spec in d-tac-lqr on two points where the AC turned out too narrow during a pre-implementation walk-through.

First, body parsing splits at any markdown heading level (h1–h6), not just `##` and below. Entries are unlikely to use `#` directly but attachments routinely do — the plan's own design.md starts with `# sdd search — design` — and the same chunker has to handle both. Generalizing to all heading levels makes the AST traversal uniform and removes a hidden assumption that would have broken silently on attachments.

Second, the prepended context preamble carries both the entry's summary first sentence (as `Entry: ...`) and the heading-chain breadcrumb (as `Breadcrumb: ...`), not just the breadcrumb. Chunks otherwise lose entry-level identity from the embedded text, weakening semantic similarity when queries are entry-broad rather than section-specific. This is the contextual-retrieval principle (Anthropic, Sept 2024) applied at two layers: section context via breadcrumb, entry context via summary first sentence. The cost is ~150 chars of preamble per chunk; the benefit is the embedder seeing what the chunk is *of*, not just where in a document it sits.

Both refinements are spec-sharpening within d-tac-lqr's direction, not direction changes. They are surfaced as a downstream augmenting directive rather than a supersession of the plan, per the augmentation pattern established in d-prc-9ti — narrow refinements warrant the lighter mechanism. The implementing agent should treat both points as extensions of the plan's chunking ACs, with the closing done signal validating these alongside the original ACs.
