Redefine the ref-kind vocabulary by principle rather than by allowed target-types, and restructure the skill, framework-concepts, and pre-flight rubric to match — full reference in the [attached note](./31-160017-d-cpt-s9j/ref-kinds.md). The set settles at eight: `grounded-in`, `builds-on`, `refines`, `addresses`, `surfaces`, `depends-on`, `required-by`, `related`. Changes from today: rename `grounds` → `grounded-in` via a read-layer transform (old value mapped on parse, nothing above the parser sees it, no history rewrite); merge `evidence` into `grounded-in` (empirical-vs-conceptual hardness is read from the target's kind, so a separate kind was target-type specialization — the same trap removed from `addresses`); add `required-by` as the forward inverse of `depends-on`; generalize `addresses` from signal-only to "act on a signal or realize a decision's commitment"; keep `surfaces` for the forward case. Three organizing ideas the reference makes explicit: a kind names why a contextual (non-status) pointer exists, chosen from what the body asserts, with `related` as the floor; kinds split into mostly-backward refs and a small forward class (`surfaces`, `required-by`) captured on the source with no back-ref needed, the kind flipping with capture order; and an inverse kind is added only when the other direction is homeless (which is why `required-by` was added and no `surfaces`-inverse is). Revises the per-ref kind taxonomy (d-tac-cs0) and feeds the selection-and-calibration directive (d-prc-2is) that polices these kinds. Validated against two graphs' real usage, including a 191-entry sister graph where the generalized `addresses` and the `grounded-in` reframe corrected real misclassifications. Closes the directionality gap (s-cpt-vxb) by deciding its proposal (add `required-by`) and its broader growth principle; the gap posed a question, which this decision answers — implementing the rename/merge/new-kind across CLI, skill, framework-concepts, and rubric is the next step as a plan.
