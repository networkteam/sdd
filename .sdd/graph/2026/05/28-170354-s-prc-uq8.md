Cross-project evidence sharpens a recurring agent failure mode: agents act on incomplete pictures, treating mentions in standing guidance or entry bodies as if they were the content itself, and don't escalate research depth even under pushback. The pattern is reported in almost every session in a separate SDD project, where a sharpened CLAUDE.md rule extension ("Research before reasoning — a hypothesis without a citation is the signal you skipped research") proved insufficient. Three interacting failure modes show up. **Mentions-as-facts**: the pointer is treated as the content; following the mention to its body is skipped. **Generative-mode shortcut**: when asked to build new structure or change code, the agent shifts into pattern-matching against training-data shapes rather than starting from what this project has. **Shallow-on-pushback**: when the user signals "I don't feel aligned" or "look better", the agent runs another shallow research pass at the same depth rather than going deeper, taking multiple iterations before depth actually escalates. The scope extends beyond capture-time — the same pattern shows up in code changes, wiki edits, and any action whose next move depends on a fact being true. This builds on the alignment-skip gap (s-prc-7hw) and pre-draft grounding gap (s-prc-6ll) by adding empirical frequency and sharper diagnostic frames, and extends the foundational text-instructions-fail insight (s-stg-3vr) with the observation that text-instruction shipping *shape* matters: principle-style guidance leaves interpretation latitude that procedural guidance — concrete steps tied to specific moments — removes. The procedural-vs-principles distinction is a directional learning at medium confidence; principle text demonstrably didn't close the gap, but procedural text isn't proven to either.
