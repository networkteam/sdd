Why don't pre-flight templates include framework-concepts.md as canonical context? They list ref kinds by name and inline a few calibration examples, but the definitional text (e.g. "Responds to a gap, question, or insight signal — the entry's purpose is to act on it" for `addresses`) lives only in framework-concepts.md. Pre-flight reasons from the closed vocabulary plus illustrative examples plus the LLM's prior knowledge, not from the canonical definition.

Two drift risks shape the question: omission drift, where templates are silent on definitional nuance the canonical doc resolves or could resolve (e.g. partial-vs-full address — pre-flight inferred a strict reading on d-prc-vlu's capture without framework text available to anchor against), and inline drift, where if templates restate definitions they can diverge from the source over time without a syncing mechanism. Templates today partly take the second risk: the refines-vs-builds-on distinguishing test is inlined in ref_meta_consistency.tmpl, separate from framework-concepts.md's coverage.

Including framework-concepts.md (or relevant sections) as system context in pre-flight prompts would eliminate omission drift on definitions. Trade-offs: prompt size (~250 lines per check), risk of the LLM over-weighting canonical text against template-specific calibration, and the design choice of which definitions are needed for which checks (full doc vs. selective inclusion).
