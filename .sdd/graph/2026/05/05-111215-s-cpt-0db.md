2026-05-05 literature scan on how instruction length and density affect LLM agent performance, with citations independently verified for source quality (Perplexity returned a mix of solid sources, weak sources, and one outright misattribution; the kept set below is what survived verification).

Key findings:

- **Instruction-count degradation is empirically measured.** ManyIFEval ("Curse of Instructions," Harada et al., U-Tokyo Matsuo Lab, ICLR 2025 submission) demonstrates a power-law decline in compliance as the number of simultaneous verifiable instructions grows on frontier models. The follow-on "When Instructions Multiply" (arxiv 2509.21051) extends with regression models for predicting degradation. AgentIF (Qi et al., Tsinghua, arxiv 2505.16944) extends to agentic settings with 707 human-annotated instructions averaging ~12 constraints each. Attention dilution is observable, not metaphor.

- **Lost-in-the-middle persists on long-context models.** The canonical finding (Liu et al. 2023, arxiv 2307.03172) is reinforced by 2025 follow-on work (arxiv 2503.06868, "Lost-in-the-Middle in Long-Text Generation"). The U-shaped positional bias improves but does not disappear at long context.

- **Reasoning-mode adherence is its own concern.** ReasonIF (Kwon et al., Stanford-affiliated, arxiv 2510.15211) shows reasoning-mode models can drop instruction-following success below 0.25 on some benchmarks — distinct from raw count or position effects.

- **Vendor architectures favor modular, on-demand loading.** Anthropic's official Skills documentation explicitly recommends progressive disclosure: metadata always loaded, SKILL.md body on invocation, bundled resources on demand.

- **What's not established**: exact thresholds (e.g. "10k tokens is too long") or universal claims that monolithic prompts are always inferior. Research supports modular-by-default with routing care and per-case empirical validation, not modular-as-prerequisite.
