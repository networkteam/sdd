First-real-use evaluation of `sdd view --layout='top(N)'` against the live graph surfaced that "what's hot" catch-up is dominated by standing entries — kinds whose nature is to be cited indefinitely (contracts, aspirations, and high-layer directives). They crowd actionable work out of the top slots even though their position reflects structural anchoring rather than current pulse.

The grammar has no negation: `kind(K1, K2)` is positive-disjunction; multiple `kind()` calls intersect. There's no `kind(!K)`, `not(...)`, or `exclude-kind(...)`. Workaround today is explicit positive enumeration of every kind except the unwanted ones — verbose and brittle as new kinds are added.

Resolution shape is open. Three families: a general `not(<filter>)` primitive (most composable, biggest parser surface), negation tokens inside `kind(...)` like `kind(!K)` (tight syntax but mixing positive and negative tokens within one call gets ambiguous when chained with intersection), or macro-level default exclusions for `top(N)` (matches catch-up intent without grammar change but makes macros opinionated about which kinds matter). The choice has implications for parser surface, primitive symmetry, and macro vs. user authority.

Companion gap to a separately-captured heat-weighting concern — the same evaluation surfaced both, but their resolutions are independent: filtering can ship without ref-qualifier metadata; heat-weighting needs that structural change first.
