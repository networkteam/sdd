An entry can name another entry in its prose without listing it in `refs` — so the relationship exists only as text, and the graph can't see it. Every graph mechanism reads `refs`, never the prose: heat, in-degree, `sdd show` chains, drill, and `expand(refs)` are all blind to a body-only mention. So a real relationship that lives only in the prose doesn't rank, doesn't surface, and can't be followed.

The live example: `d-prc-iqw` says in its body that it depends on the Engage refactor (`d-prc-kyz`), but its only `refs` entry is `s-prc-fc0`. The dependency is real to a reader and missing from the graph — and `expand(refs)`, built to catch stale dependencies, can't catch this one because there's no edge to render.

Same shape as summary-body drift (`s-cpt-tdp`): the narrative says one thing and the structured record another. There it's the summary against the body; here it's the prose against `refs`.

Proposed rule: if an entry is named in the prose, it should be in `refs` too. When no specific kind fits, use `related` — naming it at all means it's relevant, and a weak but visible edge beats an invisible one. This is the same habit from the other direction as dropping refs the body never mentions, so the `refs` list and the entries the body names stay in step. The kind then carries whether it's a real dependency (`depends-on`) or just context (`related`) — so there's no decision about whether to ref, only which kind.

Not worked out here — turning this into a directive is a follow-up session's job. Thoughts to take into that: whether tooling should enforce it (a lint flag, a capture-time nudge) and how to leave out IDs that appear only inside quoted or pasted text rather than the author's own prose.
