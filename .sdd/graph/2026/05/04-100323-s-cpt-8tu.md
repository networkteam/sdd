The implicit roadmap — what the team is focused on right now, who is involved, what's time-bound by external coordination — currently lives in heads rather than in the graph, violating the alignment aspiration (d-stg-qlt) by forcing each participant to rebuild context from scratch. As the graph grows past 400 entries, status surfaces every open entry as equally live even when only a small slice is being actively driven, and the temporal and personal dimensions are absent. The challenge: make the implicit roadmap graph-visible without falling into issue-tracker semantics — per-entry mutable assignment fields, ceremonial sprint windows, status columns — that conflict with the immutability contract (d-cpt-e1i) and the no-parallel-artifacts aspiration (d-stg-3k0).

Proposal: capture planning sessions (daily focus, weekly slice, longer-cycle roadmap) as `kind: activity` entries at the appropriate layer — process for daily/weekly, strategic or conceptual for roadmap. Each planning entry's body contains **involvement triples** — `(target-entry, [actor-canonical, ...], optional-date-range)` — recording who is engaged with what during the cadence. Refs include all triple targets so existing graph traversal works unchanged. Closure is via supersession at each cadence rather than via done signal, since plans refresh regardless of completion.

The triple architecture is the structural escape from the issue-tracker trap: trackers put assignment and dates ON the issue itself (mutable per-entry metadata that drifts as work evolves), while SDD triples put both IN the planning entry — never on the underlying gap, signal, or decision. Underlying entries stay clean; the supersession chain of plans encodes evolution. Multi-actor involvement is the default; single-owner assignment is explicitly not adopted, since SDD's existing pattern (`participants:`) is already multi-actor and reality is angled rather than owned (the Kōgen story shows Mara and Jun both contributing to the same layout decision from different angles). Prose within the planning entry names angles when they matter.

Catch-up rendering uses a three-section split: **In focus** (entries refd by active planning entries), **Warm but unmentioned** (recent ref activity but no current planning involvement — the action signal: needs driving or explicit parking), **Parked** (collapsed with count). Heat sources are dual: any recent incoming ref counts as background warmth; refs from active planning entries get first-class boost as declared focus. Personal view (`sdd status --involves me` or similar) filters active plans by triples mentioning your canonical, surfacing what you're engaged with — not what you "own."

Date placement: default is one date range on the activity entry (the plan's window); per-relation dates on individual triples are an open extension for plans spanning commitments with different ETAs. The discriminator for *whether* dates belong in the graph at all: external coordination constraint (launch announcement, partner deadline) → capture; internal manufactured cadence (sprint windows, arbitrary rhythms) → don't, per the artifacts aspiration (d-stg-3k0).

Open sub-dimensions for follow-up: (1) heat decay function — how recent must a ref be to count as warm; (2) triple storage format — frontmatter list, markdown table in body, or YAML block; (3) routing remains a sibling concern (s-cpt-5jk) — who *should* weigh in versus who is *involved*. Vision-level extension: notification on involvement change as graph syncs.

Supersedes the focus/priority layer gap (s-cpt-cca), which named the need but proposed three candidate mechanisms without resolving them. This signal absorbs cca's question and proposes the planning-activity-with-involvement-triples shape as the answer, with the structural reasoning that distinguishes it from issue-tracker semantics.
