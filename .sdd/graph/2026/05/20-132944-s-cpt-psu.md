Does SDD need explicit discipline to prevent the graph from becoming an echo chamber — where new captures ground on prior entries that themselves carried bad facts, compounding drift over time? The just-captured pre-draft grounding gap (20260520-103250-s-prc-6ll) names one mechanism by which bad facts can enter: dialogue substance echoed into entries without verification. Once such an entry lands, future captures may treat it as established context, so new captures ground on potentially unhealthy information rather than verified fact.

Supersedes is the existing retirement mechanism — bad facts and entries can be ruled out so new captures ground on healthy information — but supersedes only fires when someone notices drift and writes a corrective entry; it does not actively defend against the compounding effect. The question holds open what shape an active defense would take. A contract feels premature — the constraint to encode isn't yet clear. Other candidates: a periodic lint-style health check, a capture-time directive about grounding from active entries, a separation between "we said this" (dialogue residue) and "this is true" (verified fact), or trusting supersedes to keep up. Worth dialoguing as more instances surface.
