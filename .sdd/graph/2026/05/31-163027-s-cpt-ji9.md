Semantic conflicts between entries — two that commit to incompatible decisions or contradict each other — go undetected at scale because nothing mechanically surfaces them. Unlike a git conflict, which is self-announcing and (under the conflict-free contract proposed in s-cpt-t2n) signals broken tooling, a semantic conflict is silent: no error, no merge marker, no trigger. Today it is caught only on sight — when a participant happens to read two contradicting entries — which does not scale as the graph grows past hundreds of entries and parallel work multiplies the pairs to check. This directly threatens the coherence-across-parallel-work aspiration (d-stg-qlt): incompatible commitments can coexist unnoticed, and later entries may ground on the losing side.

**Proposed direction** (not decided): treat detected semantic conflicts as first-class signals to capture and track, and find a detection mechanism that works without a mechanical trigger — vector similarity via `sdd search` is an obvious lead for surfacing candidate contradictions for human judgment. Adjacent to the echo-chamber-drift question (s-cpt-psu), where unverified facts silently become grounding. Raised by Jonathan while sharpening the conflict-free contract proposal.
