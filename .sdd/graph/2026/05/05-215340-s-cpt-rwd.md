Entries referenced by many other entries (high in-degree) are structurally central to the graph and rank higher for lightweight catch-up surfacing. Combined with s-cpt-8tu's heat decay (ref recency), this forms a composite ranking signal: structurally important entries that are also recently active surface first.

In-degree is computable directly from the existing refs graph — no new primitives needed. The composite score gives a mechanically derived top-N without agent synthesis or clustering, providing the selection criterion the lightweight catch-up mode needs.

The heat decay function remains an open sub-dimension: linear over N days, or tied to supersession of the containing focus entry?
