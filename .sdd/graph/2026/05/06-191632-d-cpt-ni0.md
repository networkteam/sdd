The SDD graph recognizes exactly two entry types — signal and decision — each carrying an explicit kind. Entries outside these types or carrying kinds outside the 7+7 set are rejected.

**Signal kinds (7):** gap (default), fact, question, insight, done, actor, annotation.
**Decision kinds (7):** directive (default), activity, plan, contract, aspiration, role, focus.

## Per-kind structural requirements

- **Plan decisions** require a `## Acceptance criteria` section with at least one `- [ ]` checklist item.
- **Done signals** require at least one `closes` or `refs` link (the target of the completion claim).
- **Actor signals** (process layer by convention) require a `canonical` frontmatter field. Optional `aliases` list serves read-side comprehension and mining only — never resolved at capture time.
- **Role decisions** (process layer by convention) require an `actor:` frontmatter field naming the canonical of the actor-identity chain the role binds to.
- **Annotation signals** require `refs:` (the entries the annotation is about) and `topics:` (the assignments). Each `topics` item is either a label string (applies to all refs) or `{label, members}` where `members` is a subset of `refs`.
- **Focus decisions** require `involvement:` — a list of `{target, actors?, when?}` triples with target resolving to an existing entry. Optional top-level `actors:` and `when:` provide defaults; per-involvement values override. Explicit `actors: []` is meaningful (pull-available) and distinct from omitted (inherits default). `when` requires at least one of `from` / `to` (ISO date) when present.

## Topics

Topics live on every entry kind via inline `topics: [<label>, ...]`. Annotation entries provide retroactive or bulk reorganization through the `{label, members}` form. An entry's effective topic set merges its inline topics with topics declared by annotations whose refs include it.

Path representation is `[]string` of components joined by `/` at I/O. Components match `[\p{L}\p{N}\-]+` (Unicode letter, digit, or `-`); empty components rejected. Comparison is case-insensitive on each component; first-seen casing wins for display. No alias mechanism: drift is addressed by capturing a new annotation, not by rewriting history.

## Cross-kind invariants

- **Write-once canonical across actor-identity chains.** A canonical can only appear in one actor-identity chain ever; within a chain it is free. Closed chains do not free their canonicals for reuse.
- **Canonical-only in participants.** Every entry's `participants` field carries canonical names only; aliases are never accepted at capture time. Pre-flight enforces this mechanically with binary severity.
- **Role-status cascade.** A role with `actor: X` is derived-active iff the unique chain that ever held X as canonical has an active head actor signal. Retiring the chain transitively derives-closes all bound roles.
- **Annotation entries are excluded from `sdd status` kind-grouped narrative sections.** They exist to be queried, not read as observations.
- **Focus has dual lifecycle.** Retire by supersession (priorities shift mid-cycle) or by a closing done signal (cycle ended naturally). Both paths are valid; pre-flight does not argue which one applies when.

## Scope

Historical entries from before this supersession remain valid under their original kind set — immutability (`d-cpt-e1i`) prevails. Pre-flight applies kind/canonical/topic checks only to new captures. Topic membership on historical entries is forward-only: capture a new annotation rather than rewriting.
