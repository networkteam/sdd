Where is the line that makes a fact capture useful — earning a `kind: fact` entry that future captures can ground on — versus inflation of the graph with claims that don't carry their weight? The earlier grounding gap (20260520-103250-s-prc-6ll) implies a positive opportunity: if agent grounding work produces verified claims, those claims could be captured as fact signals so future sessions read them from the graph instead of repeating the research. The fact economics matter: every entry adds traversal weight, status to compute, and presentation surface area.

What counts? Candidate criteria include: a fact must be referenced or relied on by at least one captured decision or gap; a fact must come from an external source the graph can't otherwise check (vs. derivable on demand by reading code or files); a fact must sit at a stable layer (process / conceptual claims are more durable than tactical / operational ones that turn over fast). None of these is yet a settled line. The question parallels the echo-chamber concern (20260520-132944-s-cpt-psu) — both ask about graph hygiene under continued capture, but from opposite directions: echo chamber asks how to keep bad information out, fact economics asks how to let useful information in without bloat. Confidence medium — the opportunity is clear, the discipline isn't.
