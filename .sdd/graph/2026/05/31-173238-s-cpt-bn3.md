The sdd tooling (CLI and skills — the business logic) and the decision graph (the data) need clean separation, because tooling evolution can change the entry format and introduce incompatibilities: an entry written under one format may not be readable or valid under another. This is a distinct kind of conflict from the git/mechanical ones the conflict-free contract proposal (s-cpt-t2n) covers — git-clean but logically broken — so it sits outside that contract and is in tension with its "no technical conflicts" framing.

This surfaced from the proposal to pin main-branch tooling to a stable version (s-prc-vm7): pinning the tool alone doesn't make the data readable across versions. The project already holds the seed of the data/logic boundary — a single `graph_schema_version` in meta.json plus write-gating that refuses an older binary from writing, introduced with the init consolidation (d-tac-r5g) and later extended to cover init itself (d-tac-kud) — though that guard is graph-wide, one-directional (it stops old tools writing, not new tools writing entries old tools can't read), and on this graph the version floor isn't even set.

**Proposed direction** (not decided): keep the version graph-wide — a single pinned version for the whole graph — and add a mechanism to verify the whole graph conforms to its pinned version, migrating it when the version bumps, centralizing the cost in tooling at version-change time. How exactly (the check algorithm, and possibly how graph data is stored to make conformance verifiable) is open, and connects to the `sdd migrate` path that d-tac-r5g explicitly deferred ("no v2 schema yet"). Raised by Jonathan as a closing observation.
