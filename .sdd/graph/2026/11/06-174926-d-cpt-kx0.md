An entry's participants are the people whose reasoning shaped it and the people who acknowledged its consequences, each named by actor canonical. The agent drafting a capture the user confirms is not a participant.

This refines 20260911-125913-d-cpt-gso, which keeps participants so questioned entries can return to dialogue with those who did the work and reasoning. Three rules settle whom capture lists and how:

1. From a meeting or a transcript, an entry lists the people whose reasoning shaped it and the people who acknowledged its consequences, for example by agreeing to a decision. Attending does not make someone a participant. The person processing the material is listed only if they took part.
2. The agent drafting a capture that the user confirms is never listed. SDD always runs through an agent, so naming it tells a later reader nothing about whom to return to. This narrows the "people and agents" of 20260911-125913-d-cpt-gso. Agents that capture on their own, such as background agents running procedures, are a separate case this decision leaves open.
3. A participant is named by actor canonical, and the canonical may come before its actor entry. Capture accepts a name no actor declares yet, and the actor and its role can be captured later, asynchronously. Requiring the actor first would put that ceremony in front of every capture with a new collaborator. A name that is an alias of an active actor is refused with that actor's canonical, and capture serves the active actors with their aliases so the drafting agent picks the canonical in the first place. The actor kind's authoring fact (20260818-110000-s-prc-act) follows this rule.

These rules govern the fix for the missing participant list in engine capture (20260911-125948-s-tac-9cs). Served canonicals replace alias resolution in the participant checks as the answer to 20261001-150924-s-tac-td3.
