The graph has no way to record a person's or a source's picture of a subject as theirs: an understanding someone holds, stated early and possibly wrong, that contributes to a later decision without claiming to be one.

**Expected.** Stated by Christopher and Benno in a meeting on 2026-11-06. Christopher took the idea from a New York Times article on AI in creative work: much of the work is each person expressing their point of view, their understanding of the picture, to the team and the client. A point of view says neither "this is how it is" nor "this is decided". It can arrive before anything is clear enough to decide, and it may be wrong. A customer's request or an external source, such as a talk transcript fed in as grounding, carries one too. Set side by side, pictures show where they agree and where they contradict, and a contradiction should lead to a dialogue toward a shared picture. A point of view is a contribution, not a rule: it never stops anyone from acting because it contradicts their picture. It is optional, so a fact or a directive can still be captured directly. A decision that later settles the subject references it. Benno added that everyone involved in a project, project managers and customers included, should be able to contribute their picture without claiming a directive.

**Actual.** From reading the type-system facts. No kind records that an entry states someone's understanding. An insight records a synthesis connecting what the graph already holds, re-derivable from its references, and the open insights together are the project's current understanding of itself (20260816-100000-s-prc-syn). A fact is checked against the world, a gap needs an expected side, and a question asks (20260818-120000-s-prc-dsc). Participants name whose reasoning shaped an entry (20261106-174926-d-cpt-kx0), not that the entry states only their view, and confidence grades evidence, not whose view it is.

**Deviation.** A person's picture can only be recorded under a kind that claims more than it is, such as an insight that reads as the project's understanding, or it stays out of the graph.

**Unsettled candidates.** From the meeting and a follow-up dialogue between Christopher and the drafting agent.

- An insight subtype, point of view beside synthesis, keeping the current insight definition for synthesis. Re-derivability separates them: a synthesis can be rebuilt from its references, while a point of view rests on the people holding it, whom its participants name. A point of view is superseded when its holder changes their mind, and a decision that settles its subject leaves it open. Directive intent is the precedent for a parameter on a kind.
- A new signal kind. The kind set is not certain to be right, but changes to it should be resisted, which favours the subtype.
- How a contradiction between pictures reaches dialogue: as its own gap or question, or found by a view comparing points of view.
- Whether a point of view carries a further subtype, such as a comment or a statement, to show where it comes from.

A point of view is the separation between "we said this" and "this is true" that 20260520-132944-s-cpt-psu lists as a defence against echo-chamber drift, and it is what one participant believes, stated by them, which 20260604-152617-s-cpt-dnh leaves open as observer-relative state.
