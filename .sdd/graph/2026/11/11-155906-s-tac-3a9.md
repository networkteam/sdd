An entry can record only when it was written, not when the observation it records was made or the decision it records was taken, so history imported into a graph is dated, ranked and placed on timelines by its import date.

Actual: on 2026-11-11, a client project's issue-tracker history was imported into its graph as entries. Each entry carries the import date in its ID, and capture offers no field for the date or time range the material comes from. Three effects followed. Heat, which decays each reference's weight exponentially over about 14 days (exp-14d), puts the imported history at the top of catch-up for about two weeks, where it pushes out work that is actually current. `rank(by(date))` and any timeline view place the history at the import date permanently. The real time range and the ticket numbers survive only as prose in the body, which no view filter or ranking reads.

Expected: views that order or weigh entries by time use the time the recorded observation or decision belongs to. This is a natural expectation that no entry states. The graph already records a time range for one kind: a focus carries a `when` window (20260818-110300-s-prc-foc), but no other kind has such a field.

Workaround in use: every imported entry carries one common topic label, so views can filter the import in or out.

Related: the adoption gap 20260407-215428-s-stg-qg0 asks for ways to bring existing projects in, and an import is one. The example-world gap 20260716-144113-s-cpt-7ch saw timestamps that do not fit a graph's story timeline from another cause. The open heat-weighting plan 20260705-130245-d-tac-cad reworks the same heat input, and coldness (20260625-115201-s-cpt-6h6) decays by entry age as well.

Unsettled candidates, not observed: coldness may also show imported entries without inbound refs as fresh open loops. One direction is an optional field such as `observedAt` (a date or a range) that heat and date ranking use when set, while the ID keeps the write date so the log stays append-only.
