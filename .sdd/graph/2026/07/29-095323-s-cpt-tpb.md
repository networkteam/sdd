Heat does not survive supersession: `model.HeatScore` sums decayed inbound references keyed on the literal ref target, so a superseding entry starts at zero while its predecessor keeps the entire score and is simultaneously filtered out of every active lane — the attention the graph accumulated becomes unreachable at the moment the head moves.

Measured today rather than inferred. 20260727-230506-d-cpt-msg carried eight inbound references, every one captured between 27 and 29 July, so under the default `exp-14d` decay each contributes close to full weight. Its successor 20260729-092849-d-cpt-tm1 carries the same content, the same commitments and the same edges, and scores zero: it did not appear in the top twenty guiding directives, while the lane a session actually opens with shows ten. The predecessor cannot appear either, being superseded. Neither entry is wrong; the score simply stayed with an ID no reader is meant to land on any more.

The mechanism is exact and narrow. `HeatScore` in `internal/model/ranking.go` iterates `g.RefsTo[e.ID]` and adds `decay(ageDays(referencing entry))`, and `RefsTo` is built in `NewGraph` by appending each referencing entry's ID under the *literal* `ref.ID`, with no chain walk. `InDegreeScore` shares that keying, so the composite `mult`, `add` and `log` algorithms inherit it. Coldness inherits it in the opposite direction and with a worse effect: because `coldness = decay(entryAge) / (1 + inDegree)`, a fresh successor has in-degree zero and is therefore maximally cold, so a corrected entry can surface in the open-loops lane as though it were a commitment nobody has touched.

The asymmetry is what makes this a defect rather than a deliberate design choice. 20260608-102734-s-tac-4ly shipped precisely the primitive this needs: `Graph.ResolveRef` walks `SupersededBy` transitively to the live head, `DerivedStatus` resolves through it, and `sdd show` and `expand(refs)` render the trail to the head. Reference resolution and status derivation already treat the head as the thing being pointed at. Ranking is the one consumer still reading the literal target — so a reader following a ref lands on the successor, while the ranking that decides whether they ever see it credits the predecessor.

Christopher's position on the shape: the entry that superseded a previous entry should carry over some *factor* of that previous heat rather than all of it — inheritance that damps. What that leaves open is the substance, and it should be settled in dialogue rather than picked during implementation: what the factor is; whether it compounds along a chain of several supersessions or applies once from the immediate predecessor; whether it decays by the age of the supersede event as well as by the age of the original references; and whether in-degree inherits on the same terms or stays literal. Full transfer would make a re-kinding invisible in the ranking, which is attractive until a substantively new successor inherits standing it has not earned. Zero transfer is what exists now.

Where it lands, with a caution. 20260705-130245-d-tac-cad is the active plan that owns `model.HeatScore` and `model.ColdnessScore` and already reasons about a `retired` target class, so it is the natural home. But its weight table damps references pointing *at* retired targets, which lowers the predecessor's score without raising the successor's — it treats the visible half of the symptom and leaves this half untouched. Folding this in therefore needs a deliberate decision, not an assumption that the weighting work already covers it.

Proportionality, stated plainly: nothing is lost and no record is wrong, so this is not urgent. The effect is confined to ranking order in views and catch-up lanes. It bites hardest exactly where supersession is used to correct rather than to evolve — a re-kinding, an intent stamp, a wording fix — because there the successor is the same thought and deserves the same standing, and the correction currently costs it every reference it had earned.
