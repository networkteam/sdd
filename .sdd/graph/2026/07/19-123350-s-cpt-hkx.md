One assisted Codex handoff exposed a state–serve asymmetry: the evaluate instance’s persisted `anchor`, `plan`, and `widenReport` values survived replay but were absent from the resumed MCP response, which presented those names only as writable properties in `report_schema`. The response instead supplied the current procedure, step, goal, generic instructions, status, and session handle; because neither the anchored entry nor the outer-only assisted scope encoded in `plan` was visible, the new agent re-searched the anchor, treated both lenses as outstanding, and moved into inner verification. This gap is the concrete finding produced by the outer evaluation and sharpens the earlier log-only resume question: in this run the problem arose before missing conversational reasoning, because even the durable procedure fold was reconstructed internally but not projected to the agent. It supports—but does not prove—the hypothesis that other procedures can lose handoff intent whenever a current step depends on values collected earlier that its instructions do not render. The required projection remains open: full current typed state may be sufficient, while a filtered trajectory of significant reports, chooser answers, transitions, and completed ancestors may also be needed; unrelated finished instances and mechanical read or serve events may be noise. The session event log remains a procedure trajectory rather than the full dialogue, so the design question is how much durable state and sequence a new dialogue needs without pretending to reconstruct the previous conversation.
