The engine's capture procedure cannot produce a valid focus decision — it collects no involvement triples, so the write fails the graph rule that a focus must declare at least one, and the failure surfaces only as an opaque, unrecoverable gate error. A focus decision must declare at least one involvement triple — the target(s) it advances — per the graph validator (`focus decision must declare at least one involvement triple`). But the capture procedure (d-prc-cap) collects only body, kind, layer, refs, topics, confidence, and directive-only intent at its assemble step; it has no field for involvement, focus-actors, or `when`, and its assemble guidance does not even list focus among the capturable kinds. So starting capture with kind=focus is accepted, but the write gate drives the entry write with an empty involvement list and fails with the opaque "validation failed: 1 issue(s)" — naming neither the rule nor the missing field. Worse, the instance wedges at the write gate: playback is behind it, there is no adjust-from-gate, and no field exists to supply involvement, so the served flow offers no recovery. Diagnosing it required reading the Go source (graph.go, focus.go, new_entry.go) — the same push-only reach failure s-cpt-7wc records, now hit from the error-diagnosis side. This surfaced live trying to capture the base-facts/dogfood focus this session (the wedged attempt is parked). The generalization: the capture procedure covers the common signal and decision kinds but not the structurally richer ones (focus's involvement, and likely procedure, actor, role), and write-gate validation failures need to surface as an actionable re-serve — naming the issue and routing back to a step that can fix it — rather than an opaque wedge.
