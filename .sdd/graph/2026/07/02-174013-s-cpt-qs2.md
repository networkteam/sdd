The cheapest honest dialogue evidence is produced by construction, not reconstructed after the fact: run alignment as a move whose record is itself the evidence. Playback-and-confirm invites skim-and-confirm — the user nods at a restatement of what they said, no new thought emerges, and the only reportable evidence is a summary the agent could fabricate. An interview move inverts this: the agent asks one question at a time, surfaces its own assumptions for correction, and each question, the user's actual answer, and the resulting change to the draft accumulate as a structured trajectory — the exact "trajectory to alignment" the confirmation gate demands (s-cpt-icg), generated as a by-product of doing the alignment well. Observed empirically in a real session where an explicitly requested interview produced locked design decisions and a capture that needed no skim-confirm pass. This partially answers the generic-evidence-form question (s-cpt-li1) for the dialogue surface — design moves so honest evidence falls out of running them — and gives the interactivity standard (d-prc-e2y) a concrete production mode plus its missing scaling rule: an activation condition suggests the interview form when stakes are high (strategic-layer capture, supersedure of high-confidence entries, thin grounding), leaving light playback for low-stakes captures.
