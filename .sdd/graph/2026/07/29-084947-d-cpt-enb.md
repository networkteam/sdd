A `kind: directive` decision may close any decision kind, with retirement rationale in its body — the target-kind restriction in `model.validateCloses` is removed so the validator matches the retirement table the framework already documents and ships.

The validator and the documentation disagree, and the documentation is right. The retirement table in `framework-concepts` lists "directive retiring it" as a close path for directive, activity, plan and focus as well as for contract, aspiration and procedure, while `validateCloses` permits only the last three and pushes every other decision-closes-decision to `supersedes`. The gap this retires — 20260628-130140-s-prc-xb7 — recorded that contradiction in June from the directive→plan side, where a capture was forced to supersede a plan it merely retired. It recurred today from the directive→directive side: retiring two withdrawn relocation directives, which have no successor at their own altitude, was blocked by the same rule. That gap asked for the contradiction to be resolved in either direction; this settles it toward the documented behaviour, so the gap is answered rather than left open for a third instance to rediscover.

**What the rule becomes.** `closes` retires; `supersedes` replaces and carries lineage. Which of those two relations holds is the author's judgment about the work, and the validator's job is to reject what is structurally meaningless rather than to prescribe between two meaningful relations. A directive that retires a commitment nobody will carry forward is not a lineage event, and forcing it into `supersedes` fabricates a successor relationship that does not exist — which is a worse record than the one the rule was protecting.

**What stays.** A settled directive is born terminal and carries no closing edge; supersession remains its only retirement and that guard is untouched. Non-`done` signals still cannot close, except a fact or insight dissolving a question. And `done` remains the standard closer for work that actually completed — retirement-by-directive is for commitments abandoned rather than delivered, and the two are not interchangeable: reaching for a retirement directive where a done signal belongs would erase the record of what was built.

**Why the closer stays a directive.** The wider alternative — dropping the decision-closes-decision condition altogether — was considered and rejected as broader than the defect: no use is named for a plan, an activity or an aspiration retiring another decision, and `done` already covers completion. Keeping the closer a directive preserves the documented reading that retirement is something decided, and keeps the close-pattern enumeration in the pre-flight rubric meaningful rather than vacuous.

Role is the one decision kind whose documented close path is the actor-identity cascade rather than a directive. It is nonetheless not excluded by rejection: a directive closing a role is permitted and merely unusual. A special case forbidding it would rest on no stated basis, and the cascade is a derivation that keeps working regardless.

**Surfaces in scope.** `validateCloses` and its doc comment in `internal/model/graph.go`, whose comment currently states the narrow rule as though it were the contract; the `TestValidateCloses` cases pinning it; the sanctioned-pattern enumeration in the pre-flight `unusual_close` template, which still names "directive→stable-kind retirement" as the whole permitted set; and two skill-text places repeating the narrow set — the groom playbook's stable-kind close instruction and the retirement-rationale sentence in `framework-concepts`. The rationale requirement widens with the rule rather than staying pinned to stable kinds: stating *why* a commitment is retired does not depend on the target's kind, and it matters most exactly where a commitment is abandoned unbuilt.

This lands on the line opened by 20260420-140143-s-tac-020, which relaxed the validator to admit contract and aspiration retirement in the first place and recorded that widening as implicit scope found while making a rationale check reachable; `procedure` was added later by the same reasoning. This is that reasoning carried to its end rather than a new direction — which is also why the fix is small and the entry is mostly about why the restriction was never principled.
