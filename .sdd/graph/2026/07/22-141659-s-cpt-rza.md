The engine gives the agent no served, kind-specific knowledge for composing entries correctly, so it drafts them wrong and recovers through supersedes, restarts, and overrides. This is general — every entry kind needs construction guidance the engine does not push, and some kinds are far more complex than the plain signal/decision case (procedures need substantial design guidance; plans and specs need an authoring arc) — but it bit hardest on actor and role capture across two live bootstrap runs, where it was the dominant friction. The identity misses were consistent: the canonical drafted from a full personal name instead of matching the configured participant, aliases not collected as part of the actor cluster, the canonical not introduced in the entry prose (which a quality check then flags), the actor drafted at the wrong layer, and roles not referencing the active actor head — each forcing a supersede or restart, in one run a cascade long enough to frustrate the user. The legacy bootstrap skill carried explicit steps for the identity case (confirm the configured canonical up front, collect aliases, split identity from role); the engine port dropped them with no replacement. This is the composition-side instance of the engine's push-only knowledge gap (20260707-135618-s-cpt-7wc); its committed answer is the base-facts pull layer (20260707-140544-d-cpt-dtv) plus kind-specific assemble guidance the agent reads before drafting. Identity kinds concretely need canonical-from-configured-participant, alias collection, introduce-canonical-in-prose, role-to-active-actor-head reference, and the process layer. Surfaced by the outer evaluation of the bootstrap port (20260722-140955-s-tac-lgc).
