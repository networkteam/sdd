Engine handover and resumption must preserve agent-visible fidelity of durable, intent-bearing procedure state: persistence without faithful projection into the re-entry serve is not continuity. A handoff starts a new dialogue; it does not resume the previous conversation or claim to reconstruct dialogue context. Procedure authors must therefore maintain enough current intent in typed durable state for that new dialogue to re-establish the work, including the anchor, user-chosen scope or lens, plan, collected judgments and evidence, pending decisions, and any other values on which the current step’s meaning depends. The engine must make that state available to the newly attached or reoriented agent, or explicitly identify material it omitted or compressed; intent-bearing state must never disappear silently.

This standing invariant addresses the concrete state–serve asymmetry observed in 20260719-123350-s-cpt-hkx and refines the session-model contract in 20260718-131626-d-cpt-9of, whose fidelity expectation says recorded state transfers while conversational context does not. The observed evaluate handoff demonstrates why the distinction matters: `anchor`, `plan`, and `widenReport` were durable but unavailable to the new agent, so the failure occurred before any question of recovering unrecorded dialogue.

This directive deliberately leaves the projection shape open. Serving the full current typed state may be sufficient; a filtered trajectory of semantically significant reports, chooser answers, transitions, and completed ancestors may also be needed where sequence explains present intent. Conversely, indiscriminately replaying every event risks noise from finished instances and mechanical reads or serves. A later design decision must settle that boundary, but neither compression nor filtering may silently erase the durable intent required to start the new dialogue around the current work.
