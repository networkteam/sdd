The public runtime silently discards operational errors in durable-transition and workflow-completion paths, violating the repository's fail-loud rule. The inner evaluation (20260713-165141-s-tac-78g) found this in the implementation governed by (20260712-234500-d-tac-x1m): when mutation-intent persistence fails after staged blobs were retained, ApplyPrepared calls StagedBlobs.Release and discards its error before returning only the session-append failure, so a retention leak can go unreported. Workflow Advance also discards ServeShell failure after a move completes, while Abandon and Park do the same after changing move state; callers can receive apparent success with the expected base serve missing and the underlying access, holder, or storage failure hidden. These are not best-effort diagnostics—the cleanup and shell transition are part of the operation's durable or user-visible result. The paths need explicit error combination or typed recovery errors, with regression tests that inject both primary and cleanup/serve failures and verify none are reduced to logs or zero-value fields.
