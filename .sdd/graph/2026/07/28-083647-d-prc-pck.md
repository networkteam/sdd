Acceptance criteria must declare what can be evaluated from outside the work, and both evaluation lenses then judge that declaration: inner whether the architecture and code serve it and are done right, outer whether it is observable in real use and how well it actually serves — a graded judgment, not a tick.

**Authoring: criteria live at the surface.** An acceptance criterion states an outcome observable from outside the artifact — what a user or a calling agent can see, read, or do — not the internal structure that produces it. Internal shape may still be constrained where a project's own guidelines demand it (this repo's CQRS decomposition is such a case), but that is a design constraint, not an acceptance criterion. The reason is mechanical rather than stylistic. 20260726-204112-s-prc-ogp establishes that for any outcome whose truth lives outside the artifact, naming an inspection is not naming a check; a criterion phrased about internals has no outside at all, so no confrontation can reach it. It is satisfiable by the same understanding that produced it — which is precisely why review rounds certify it and first use breaks it. The freshest instance is 20260728-081252-s-tac-tif: an internally-phrased outcome — the projection derives actionability from the recorded apply outcome — passed `go vet`, the full suite and the reviewer's own reasoning, while the surface it existed to fix still read `delivered · recovered` on all 122 live rows; building the binary and reading its output is what caught it.

**Judging: both lenses, with different subjects.** A surface-declared criterion is judged from both sides of the two-lens model in 20260703-200000-d-prc-evl, and neither side substitutes for the other. The inner lens asks whether the architecture and the code actually serve the declared criteria and are done right — soundness in service of the criteria rather than soundness as an independent virtue. The outer lens asks whether the outcome is observable from outside and how it feels in real use: does it work for real, and is the user or agent it serves genuinely well served. The outer judgment is graded, not binary. Terminal UX is the standing example — it is always somewhat sub-par, and a tick hides the whole question of how well it serves. This takes the validation-shape rule of 20260419-121939-s-cpt-wiv, which paired contracts with binary compliance and aspirations with gradient alignment, and extends the gradient posture to criteria satisfaction: the point is to make "met, but poorly" expressible, since a verdict with nowhere to go silently becomes "met".

What this deliberately does not do: enumerate what counts as the outside for a given project. Per 20260726-204112-s-prc-ogp that stays project-supplied — the base procedures carry the distinction and the demand, rules carry what counts as reality here.

It works the same seam as 20260726-200257-s-prc-cbl from the other end: that insight asks for a proportionality gate judging defensive fixes at review time, while surface-declared criteria remove the authoring-side pretext — when no criterion mandates internal shape, internal machinery must justify itself on its own merits instead of inheriting a plan's authority.

The surfaces this demands are named, not left implicit: the plan-authoring procedure asked for by 20260706-215247-s-prc-38f must carry the surface-declaration rule, and the lens definitions in 20260703-200000-d-prc-evl must carry the sharpened subjects — inner judging architecture and code in service of the declared criteria, outer judging observable behaviour and felt quality on a gradient. Recorded as pending rather than guiding for that reason — the failure pattern diagnosed in the dialogue that produced this entry is a decision taken while no mechanical surface changes, and a standing-context posture here would repeat it.
