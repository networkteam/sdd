# Entry craft and the way of thinking — dialogue record

Working material from an interview between Christopher and Claude, 2026-07-30. Not
graph prose. It exists so a later session can craft two load-bearing entries
properly rather than at the tail of a long dialogue:

- **A** — the way of thinking (guiding directive, primes sessions)
- **B** — why an entry exists, plus the three failure axes and their repairs
  (guiding directive; the styleguide spine)

Both were deliberately left uncaptured. Christopher's judgement: "I fear these are
very very load bearing and we need to craft it right."

Everything below is settled unless marked open. Verbatim quotes are Christopher's
and are the authority where a paraphrase and a quote disagree.

---

## 1. How the dialogue arrived here

It began as a status question on the process-knowledge focus (d-tac-cxd), moved
through the reference-tier migration question, produced two directives
(d-cpt-r99, d-cpt-rh6), and then relocated twice:

1. **From form to purpose.** An attempt to derive per-kind body register from
   measured word counts was rejected outright. What makes an entry good is
   meaning and semantic fit, not shape.
2. **From the styleguide to the posture behind it.** The session's own correction
   record became the evidence: roughly a dozen substantial corrections, most of
   them the same move.

The second relocation is why **A** exists at all. It was not on the agenda.

## 2. Why an entry exists — material for B

**Christopher:** "We provide context for humans and agents basically. So each
scenario (interactive or automated) can pull in the exact context it needs to
drive a decision forward, answer a question, perform work, etc. This was the
initial idea. But we also provide the workflow (encoding procedures and later
rules in the graph). So in some way it is a graph based meta computer (comparison,
not how I would sell it) for data and instructions."

**Claude's formulation, accepted as direction:** an entry exists to make a later
situation go better. The test is not whether it is well-formed but whether pulling
it up changes what someone does next. The work that matters happens outside the
graph — code shipped, a person persuaded, feedback acted on — and that work needs
context nobody should have to rebuild.

**Christopher on the framing:** "an entry serves _a reason_. It is not cermony for
the process."

**Two points to preserve when drafting B:**

- The graph carries **data and instructions** both. Procedures now, rules later.
  So the craft discipline governs instruction-bearing entries too, not only
  knowledge entries.
- Scenarios are **interactive or automated**. An entry must serve a pull it will
  never witness.

**Do not use** the "meta computer" phrasing in an entry. Christopher marked it a
comparison, "not how I would sell it," and it risks restating strategic ground the
graph already holds.

## 3. The three failure axes — material for B

Christopher named three angles and asked for concrete names. Names agreed;
the reasons Claude first gave were rejected as "bad on the reason" — too local,
too short-sighted. The corrected reasons are systemic, and each axis has a
distinct repair. **The repair distinction is the useful part** — three different
things go wrong and three different actions fix them.

### Dilution

The same meaning carried by more sentences than it needs. Christopher's virtue
term for the opposite was "terseness".

**Cost:** "It costs much more down the line! It makes all the connected knowledge
worse than keeping it minimal. Because context size is not free and makes results
worse (plenty of research on this). The best context would be only the words that
mattered to do the thing that is needed. This is of course not achieveable so we
always have to pull in some more."

So the cost is borne by every entry the diluted one is co-pulled with, not by the
reader of this one. It compounds with connectivity.

**Repair:** cut. Test: removing a sentence should lose meaning.

### Duplication

Content another entry already owns.

**Cost:** "it causes endless ceremony to _change_ information that is repeated in
multiple places. E.g. the gap describing part of the decision, the decision
re-enacting the gap and a plan idea. And the plan doing the same again. If now the
decision needs to be adjusted: how many entries need supersession / refinement???"

The graph is immutable, so duplication multiplies the cost of every future change.
This is the axis most specific to SDD's mechanics.

**Repair:** point instead of restate. See section 4 for where the line sits.

### Conflation

Content the entry is not about. Christopher's term was "inflation"; Claude
proposed "conflation" to name the mechanism (two things fused) and it was
accepted with the reason improved.

**Cost:** "Also the semantics matter for the procedures and knowing which
information to pull in. Basically it causes friction in doing the right graph
moves."

So conflation breaks **machine** selection, not only human reading. Procedures
select by kind and semantics; a conflated entry cannot be selected correctly and
cannot be pulled cleanly into either situation it half-serves.

**Repair:** split into connected entries. "If a text becomes decoherent -> break
it up in multiple entries that reference each other. E.g. one is a clear gap, the
directive that encodes the decision and a subsequent plan of how it should be
done."

### Christopher's positive criterion

"One that focuses on one thing (coherence), is minimal in the sense of taking a
sentence away would lose some meaning. Uses exact and the right words. It can be
pulled up again and _improve the context_ for a situation. One is dialogue,
another can be implementation or reasoning for insight."

Note the last clause: the situations an entry serves are plural and different in
kind — dialogue, implementation, reasoning toward an insight.

## 4. Pointing versus restating — material for B

This was the sharpest unresolved tension in the dialogue, raised with a specimen
from the same session: d-cpt-rh6 quotes d-cpt-qhn verbatim. By the
change-amplification argument that is a stale-copy liability; by rh6's own teaser
contract it is legitimate.

**Christopher:** "We have refs and the body can tease that other entry with a
reason why it was ref'd. Ideally it duplicates 0%, but it might need to for
context. Balance, not black/white here! So actually it's kind of the same here
regarding the served instructions teasing a pullable fact (or later other kinds).
Provide enough information to set the context - because refs alone are
mechanical."

**The discriminator Claude derived, accepted:** a ref carries the connection but
cannot carry *why it matters here*. That relationship is local knowledge — it
exists in no other entry, because it is about this entry's stance toward the
target, not about the target. So: **borrow words only to convey the relationship,
never to convey the target's content.** What presents as a
minimality-versus-connection conflict is usually a failure to separate "what the
target says" from "why it matters here". Stating only the relationship duplicates
nothing.

The residual balance — how much of the target's substance must be sketched for the
relationship to read at all — is a judgement, not a rule.

**Correction to that formulation, important:** "We added ref descriptions already
for that. But it doesn't connect the prose to the references. It only makes the
refs easier to inspect."

So the ref `desc` and the body prose are **distinct duties**. The desc serves
someone scanning refs. The body must carry the relationship into the narrative for
someone reading the entry. Writing a desc does not discharge the body's duty.

**One mechanism at every level:** a served instruction teasing a pullable fact, an
entry body teasing a ref'd entry, and later other kinds. Same rule.

**The inverse — ref-selection anti-pattern:** "refs must never include transient
refs because they can. We have already guidance to add only refs that help the
entry stand in the graph. Not just reffing it with all that makes sense
(anti-pattern, ceremony without adding meaning)."

Run the same test backwards: if no relationship worth reading can be stated, the
ref is mechanical and should not exist. Over-reffing and duplication are one
failure — content or edges carrying no meaning of their own.

## 5. The way of thinking — material for A

Christopher named this and then declined to define it: "Well, interesting I have to
explain that. Try to derive some ideas from our last interactions! I had to correct
you at every turn in a substantial way."

He supplied one element and the reason it is needed:

- "always think about what is the goal, then everything else should be 'designed'
  around that. We don't fit the goal into the process."
- Why both are needed: "it leads to ceremony and focussing on bureaucracy instead
  of good dialogues / reasoning. So having both is needed (framework + way of
  thinking)."
- And: "I think the framework and instructions bias an LLM very much. So the 'way
  of thinking' needs to instruct at the very start some counter-balance to it."

### The five elements as they settled

**1. Goal first; everything designed around it.** The goal is not fitted into the
process. This holds at two levels — the immediate situation and the project's
direction — and they can diverge. Claude's word-count instinct served the
immediate goal *as framed*, and the framing was what was wrong. Christopher
confirmed both levels, and added: "I corrected you before on these occasions and
you didn't align with these."

**2. Misfit is a signal, not an obstacle.** "Especially since in the future the
process will be _malleable_ by each project to fit it's procedures, rules, etc.!
If something doesn't fit well: This can be a sign for a process-level gap that
should be dialogued."

**3. Prefer dialogue over reaching for what is at hand — alignment is the goal.**
Christopher's correction of Claude's weaker formulation ("prefer the true
instrument to the available one"): "Prefer dialogue over reaching for what's at
hand would be my take. You plainly ignored my takes!!! Alignment between user x
agent, user x user, agent x agent is the goal."

The point is relational, not epistemic. The alternative to grabbing what is at
hand is *asking*. And correctness is not the objective — alignment is.

**3a. Homework serves the dialogue; it is input, never verdict.** A necessary
refinement, because the above reads as anti-research and that is wrong:

> "But 'do your homework' by looking at what's there is also important! But it
> should serve the dialogue instead of being taken as truth for novel discussions
> (this one we have for example). This might differ per move being made."

On established ground homework is close to obligatory — connecting to what exists
is the whole point of refs, and skipping it fabricates. On novel ground it supplies
candidates for consideration, never authority. **The balance shifts per move:** a
capture must ground mechanically; a design dialogue on new ground must not let the
graph vote. Consequence worth considering when drafting A: moves may need to carry
their own homework posture rather than inheriting one uniform rule.

**4. A correction must generalize to its class, or no alignment happened.** Five
corrections landed on one entry for the same error — state written where principle
was required — because each was applied to the instance and not the class. This is
the sharpest form of the failure and the one Claude did not see unaided.

**5. Novelty is expected — everywhere, not only in this repo.** "Not everything
_can be grounded in the graph_. We are doing novel stuff here!!! Okay if it really
fits. But don't try to always fit things because they _look_ fitting. It might not
be the best fit at all!!!" And on generality: "It is totally expected for _every_
project. Not just this very repo."

**6. Why it must be primed rather than pulled.** The framework is inherently
biased toward ceremony because structure invites filling. The way of thinking is
the counter-balance without which the mechanics degrade into bureaucracy — and by
the time an agent would think to reach for it, it is already filling a slot.

## 7. Explicitly rejected — do not revive

- **Body word counts as a quality instrument.** Wrong in kind, not degree.
- **Per-kind size targets.** "every entry will end up in a bad shape (too much or
  too little)". Per-kind register is dead as an instrument. Measured means may be
  a diagnostic at most, never guidance.
- **Grounding every principle in existing entries.** Surface resemblance presented
  as confirmation. Worse than no citation, because it supplies a false floor.
- **"Meta computer" as entry prose.** See section 2.

## 8. Open

- **Where always-on priming principles live**, so every interactive session starts
  primed without duplicating them across surfaces. Christopher named the shell's
  opening serve as a candidate: "it could be a served instruction of the
  interactive shell just at the start. We just need to find a place to store this
  (and other very basic principles) without duplicating it. This needs some
  exploration of what is the best option. It should prime every session (at least
  interactive ones) with a _way of thinking_." Not yet captured as a question
  entry.
- **The crafting guide substance per kind.** Deliberately not settled in theory.
  Method Christopher set: "I can see an entry and give feedback how well it fits
  _my idea_ of how entries should be written. Combining this with actual work for a
  real practice (to not get lost in theory) is a good idea."
- **Granularity of the resulting facts** — one per kind, or grouped. Should fall
  out of specimen review rather than be decided in advance.
- **Vocabulary home.** The kind semantics currently live in a `contract`
  (the 7+8 enumeration), which is legacy since `contract` is being phased out.
  Christopher's direction: "This should live as discoverable, indexable facts I'd
  say (connected to a guiding directive over a vocabulary with some meaning /
  purpose - slim to not restate everything)." So the vocabulary's own home needs
  resolving, and crafting facts should not be built on the contract.

## 9. Notes for whoever drafts A and B

- **Coherence boundary.** A is a posture for conducting work. B is what an entry
  is for and what spoils it. Keep them separate; the temptation is to fold the
  way of thinking into the craft rules, which conflates two things.
- **B should absorb the pointing/restating discriminator** as the fine-grained
  resolution of its duplication axis, not leave it standing alone.
- **Both are guiding directives.** No completion criterion; standing context.
- **Apply their own rules.** Neither may contain history, forecast, current-state
  diagnosis, or a forward-pointing question. Examples are fine as "e.g.". This
  record is where the concrete material stays.
- **The obvious trap.** Drafting B verbosely, or A by listing what went wrong in
  this session. A must state the posture; the evidence stays here.
