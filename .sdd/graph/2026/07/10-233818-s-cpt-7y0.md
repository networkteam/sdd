SDD cannot turn a dependency-side dialogue into a native upstream work item: when repository B declares repository A as a dependency and a dialogue in B discovers a change needed in A, the session can read A but can capture only into B. The one-way independent-write boundary was deliberate in the original cross-repo design (20260529-131915-d-cpt-v8t), and the active discovery design (20260606-003344-d-cpt-s6q) makes A readable from B without introducing shared write coordination. The delivered dependency structure (20260708-185833-d-tac-nhx) sharpens the problem: B may refer to A, while a native entry created in A cannot refer back to B without violating the declared dependency direction.

Switching the user to a separate session in A requires the originating dialogue and its reasoning to be restated or manually transported. Capturing the need only in B preserves that reasoning, but leaves the required work outside A's native graph and therefore outside the place where A's participants plan, prioritize, and close it. Read federation stops at the exact point where dialogue produces upstream work, creating the handoff friction the coherence aspiration (20260422-122317-d-stg-qlt) pushes against.

The missing mechanism must preserve A's ownership of writes and B's originating context without silently modifying a cached dependency checkout or weakening the one-way dependency invariant. Candidate shapes remain open: a deliberate target-repository capture against an authorized writable checkout, a portable handoff that A explicitly accepts into its graph, or an incoming-request/backlink view derived from B. This signal identifies the boundary problem without choosing among them.
