The `contract` kind was deprecated on 22 June and the deprecation has reached no capture surface, so standing constraints keep being captured as contracts — most recently 20260727-230506-d-cpt-msg on 27 July — and every contract so captured is structurally excluded from the standing-context lane each session opens with.

The deprecation is recorded and unambiguous. 20260622-084244-d-tac-eho deprecates `contract`, and its second acceptance criterion states the capture-side half plainly: the skill steers new captures to rules, existing contracts stay valid, the kind set becomes a transitional enumeration. Neither half shipped. The `rule` kind does not exist in the model — `internal/model/entry.go` enumerates `contract` and no `rule` — and the bundled skill still steers toward the deprecated kind: "Use `--kind contract` for standing constraints", and, pointing the wrong way outright, "A directive that hardens into a permanent rule can be reclassified later via supersedes + kind: contract". An agent capturing a standing constraint today is therefore *following the guidance in force* when it picks `contract`. That is what makes this drift invisible: it is guidance-conformant, so no reviewer and no pre-flight check has anything to object to.

The discriminator has moved twice since, and neither move reached a surface either. 20260608-235644-s-cpt-7ca dissolved the overloaded kind into two existing homes — situational constraints become binding rules, always-on commitments become guiding directives — and 20260629-001157-s-cpt-c7f corrected the axis from situational-vs-always-on to activation-vs-stance: a rule carries a "when" that fires and can be checked, a guiding directive is a persistent stance with nothing to fire. Under either reading the instance is a guiding directive — a standing stance on how graph mutation delivery behaves, governing SDD's own code and tooling, which is precisely what 20260628-154618-d-cpt-dym reserves `intent: guiding` for.

The cost is measurable, not aesthetic, and it was observed live in the session that produced this signal. The dialogue shell 20260704-100000-d-prc-dlg declares its standing-context lane as `kind(directive):intent(guiding):active`. A contract can never match that filter, and contracts carry no `intent` field at all, so the exclusion is not fixable per entry — it follows from the kind. The session that engaged 20260727-230506-d-cpt-msg opened without it in the guiding lane, while the calibration it answers to, a directive, was present. The same holds for all nine active contracts. A standing constraint that cannot appear as standing context is a decision the graph holds but never surfaces — and unsurfaced standing constraints are how the machinery in that contract's own subject area grew unchallenged.

The reason nothing moved is the coupling, and that is the part worth acting on. 20260622-084244-d-tac-eho makes the capture-side steer part of the rule-system delivery, so the steer waits on a new kind, its validation, its activation block, its pre-flight integration and its rendering. But the triage in 20260608-235644-s-cpt-7ca routes a substantial share of contracts — the always-on stances rather than the situational checks — to guiding directives, a kind that exists and ships today. That half needs no rule system: it needs the capture guidance to stop offering `contract` for standing constraints and to name the discriminator instead. Whether to decouple it that way, or to hold the whole steer until rules land, is the decision this gap asks for — with the evidence that holding has already cost five weeks and at least one mis-kinded entry.

Two distinct pieces of work follow and should not be conflated: the systemic one above, and the concrete correction of 20260727-230506-d-cpt-msg by superseding it as a guiding directive with its content intact. The concrete correction does not wait on the systemic fix, and doing it alone would leave the source open.
