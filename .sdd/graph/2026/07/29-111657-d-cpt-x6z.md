Ranking resolves every inbound reference to the live head of its supersede chain, and each hop damps that reference's heat contribution by a factor — so a superseding entry inherits the attention its predecessor earned, reduced rather than reset or transferred whole.

**The rule.** A reference's contribution to heat becomes `decay(refAge) × hopFactor^hops`, where `hops` is the number of supersede steps between the literal ref target and the live head — zero for a reference pointing directly at a live entry, so nothing changes for the ordinary case. Resolution applies uniformly across every ranking consumer: in-degree counts references resolved to the head, which is what pulls a fresh successor out of the coldness lane, since `coldness = decay(entryAge) / (1 + inDegree)` reads in-degree zero as maximally cold. The hop factor damps heat only; in-degree stays a count, because a fractional count has no meaning. If the weighted in-degree of 20260705-130245-d-tac-cad ships, whether the hop factor also multiplies there is that plan's call to state rather than inherit silently.

**Starting factor: 0.8 per hop, deliberately provisional.** One correction keeps four fifths of its standing, so a re-kinding or an intent stamp stays visible where it was; three hops fall to about half, so a long evolution chain damps without erasing. The value is a calibration guess, not a derivation, and it follows the precedent of 20260705-130245-d-tac-cad — a deliberately small, revisable table validated by comparative findings on the live graph at closure rather than argued in advance.

**Why references and not a score.** The rejected alternative was giving the successor `factor × HeatScore(predecessor)` as its own term. It is simpler read in isolation and worse everywhere else: it is a second mechanism standing beside the resolution primitive that already exists, it leaves in-degree and coldness needing their own parallel rules, and it makes heat mean two different things depending on whether an entry has a predecessor. Reference inheritance fixes heat, in-degree, coldness and the `mult`/`add`/`log` composites in one place, because they all read the same resolved index.

**Two knobs deliberately not added.** The age of the supersede event is not a separate term: the inherited references keep decaying by their own ages, so a correction inheriting three-week-old references correctly reads as cool while a re-kinding of something referenced yesterday correctly reads as warm. That behaviour falls out of the model rather than needing a rule, and adding a second decay would be a knob with no failure it prevents. And there is no per-hop distinction between a correction and a substantive evolution: nothing in the graph marks which a supersede was, so one factor serves both, and the factor is set where a correction stays visible because that is the case the current behaviour damages most.

**Scope.** Supersession only. A closed entry has no successor, so nothing inherits across `closes` — the closing entry earns its own references. Retirement remains retirement.

**Where it lands, and what tells us it worked.** `model.HeatScore` and the `RefsTo` keying in `NewGraph` are the implementation sites, reusing `Graph.ResolveRef` from 20260608-102734-s-tac-4ly rather than duplicating the walk. The observable check is at the surface, not in the function: superseding an entry keeps its successor in the lanes a reader would expect to find it, and the instance that surfaced this — a convergence rule re-kinded from contract to guiding directive, absent from a top-twenty guiding lane it should have led — must appear in that lane afterwards. A unit test proving the arithmetic is necessary and not sufficient.

This retires 20260729-095323-s-cpt-tpb by answering it. That gap named four open questions — the factor, chain compounding, whether the supersede's own age counts, and whether in-degree inherits — and each is settled above: 0.8 provisionally, compounding as `hopFactor^hops` by construction, no separate supersede-age term with the reason stated, and in-degree inheriting resolution but not damping. Nothing in the gap is deferred, so it closes rather than staying open behind a partial answer.
