The SDD graph recognizes exactly two entry types — signal and decision — each carrying an explicit kind; this revision adds the `procedure` decision kind in a transitional 7+8 enumeration, with `rule` designed alongside but deferred. It supersedes the 7+7 contract because the workflow-engine line turns playbook moves into graph entries, and the kind set must admit them before the engine can load one. Procedure and rule were designed together in one type-system revision so both arrive through the same serving mechanism, but only procedure ships now — rule joins the enumeration when the rule-system plan delivers it as a fast-follow behind the engine spine. Contract's planned folding into rule is explicitly out of scope here and lands with that work.

**Signal kinds (7):** gap (default), fact, question, insight, done, actor, annotation.
**Decision kinds (8):** directive (default), activity, plan, contract, aspiration, role, focus, procedure.

## Per-kind structural requirements

Carried forward unchanged from the superseded contract:

- **Plan decisions** require a `## Acceptance criteria` section with at least one `- [ ]` checklist item.
- **Done signals** require at least one `closes` or `refs` link.
- **Directive decisions** require an explicit `intent` (pending | guiding | settled) at capture — no default.
- **Actor signals** (process layer) require a `canonical` frontmatter field; optional `aliases` serve read-side comprehension only.
- **Role decisions** (process layer) require an `actor:` frontmatter field naming the canonical of the actor-identity chain the role binds to.
- **Annotation signals** require `refs:` and `topics:`; each topics item is a label string or `{label, members}` with members a subset of refs.
- **Focus decisions** require `involvement:` triples with resolving targets; explicit `actors: []` (pull-available) stays distinct from omitted (inherits default).

New in this revision:

- **Procedure decisions** are pinned to the process layer like actor and role — a procedure defines how we work — and require a `canonical` frontmatter field: the move's stable identity (capture, engage, …). Everything binds to the canonical, never the entry ID. Frontmatter carries the move's state machine (typed `params`/`state` declarations and `steps` naming functions from a closed Go registry); the body carries per-step instruction units. Structural validation of the spec itself (registry names, guard grammar, step shape) is the engine's load-time job, not a capture-time frontmatter check.

## kind: rule — designed now, ships fast-follow

Per the rule-system plan: required `enforcement` (binding | advisory) and an `activation` block (`when` for semantic discovery, `matches` for mechanical selection). In engine terms a rule is an attachment that arrives from the graph instead of the procedure spec: binding rules add predicates at matching steps, advisory rules add instruction units. The step model is rule-ready from day one; v1 base procedures declare their checks in the spec.

## Topics

Unchanged: topics live on every entry kind via inline `topics:`; annotation entries provide retroactive or bulk assignment; effective topic set merges inline and annotation-declared labels. Path components match `[\p{L}\p{N}\-]+`, comparison case-insensitive per component, first-seen casing wins. No alias mechanism — drift is addressed by new annotations, not rewriting history.

## Cross-kind invariants

Carried forward: write-once actor canonicals across chains; canonical-only participants (pre-flight enforced, binary severity); role-status cascade from the bound actor chain; focus dual lifecycle (supersede mid-cycle or close by done); annotation entries excluded from kind-grouped narrative renderings.

New in this revision:

- **Write-once procedure canonical.** Procedure canonicals form their own namespace, separate from actor canonicals, with the same write-once rule: a canonical may only ever appear in one procedure supersession chain; closed chains do not free their canonicals.
- **Base procedures and the fork rule.** Base procedures ship embedded in the sdd binary — always loaded into every graph, correct independent of graph content. A newer sdd version ships successor entries; a project customizes by superseding a chain head through normal capture discipline. When a shipped base successor and a project override compete for a chain's head, the project head wins for execution; lint flags the fork as a grooming candidate, and resolution is deliberate, merge-style — never automatic.
- **Procedure retirement.** Supersede with a revised procedure (the customization path); a directive may close a procedure head to retire the move without replacement — extending the existing directive→{contract, aspiration} retirement carve-out to procedure.

## Vocabulary

The kind name is `procedure` — "process procedure decision" reads formally on every rendered surface. Dialogue vocabulary stays "playbook move" / "move"; frontmatter, tools, and logs say procedure. A running execution is a procedure instance.

## Scope

Historical entries remain valid under their original kind set — immutability prevails; kind checks apply to new captures. Confidence is medium while procedure is unvalidated in use: the enumeration hardens — or revises — as the engine slices dogfood it.
